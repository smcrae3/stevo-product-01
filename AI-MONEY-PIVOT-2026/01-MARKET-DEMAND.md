# 01 — Market Demand (September 2026 Evidence)

Location note: this research ran in a cloud container, not on Steven's Windows
machine. `C:\Users\smcra\Documents\Stevo-AI-Workspace` was not reachable and
was not needed — this file is public-market evidence only, gathered via live
web search.

Everything below separates **people talking about making money** from
**people actually buying**, per instruction. Where a source is a "how to

## Automation / integration services (Upwork, Fiverr)

**People actually buying — yes, confirmed.**

- Job postings on Upwork name specific tools (HubSpot, Airtable, Notion,
  Slack, Sheets, Make, Zapier, n8n, OpenAI, Claude, Supabase) with a
  concrete operational ask: reduce manual work, speed up follow-up, clean
  data, route leads. [remoteaitools.com](https://remoteaitools.com/upwork-ai-automation-jobs-2026/)
- Entry-level Upwork automation rates: **$25–$40/hr or flat-rate
  equivalent**. The gap between "can talk about AI" and "can ship AI that
  survives a client's messy systems" is where better rates live — meaning
  the commodity end (prompt writing, basic labeling) is getting
  competitive and pushing rates down, while shipped, working automation
  still commands real money. [remoteaitools.com](https://remoteaitools.com/upwork-ai-automation-jobs-2026/)
- Fiverr: single Make.com/n8n scenario builds price **$100–$300**;
  "complete workflow" listings range $45–$520; AI integration project
  rates run $500–$2,500 with repeat business reported as common.
  [Fiverr cost guide](https://www.fiverr.com/resources/guides/costs/ai-automation-experts)
- This directly validates the price band Steven is already using
  ($99–$199). It is not overpriced or underpriced for the market — it's
  in range.

**Verdict:** real, currently-transacting demand. Not hypothetical.

## Dropshipping / TikTok Shop

**People actually buying** (end consumers) — yes, at scale. **People
actually profiting as sellers** — mixed, evidence-thin for beginners.

- TikTok Shop GMV: ~$33B (2024) → ~$66B (2025) → projected >$112B (2026).
  Real, large, growing consumer spend. [SaleHoo](https://www.salehoo.com/learn/tiktok-dropshipping)
- Beginner seller net margins in the **first 90 days: roughly -5% to +5%**
  — most are unprofitable during the testing phase.
  [Kixmon](https://kixmon.com/blog/is-tiktok-shop-dropshipping-profitable/)
- Even successful sellers report net margins of only 10–25% after ad
  spend, referral fees, FBT charges, and refund admin.
  [ZIK Analytics](https://www.zikanalytics.com/blog/is-dropshipping-still-profitable/)
- This category's content is dominated by "how to" guides monetizing the
  dream of dropshipping itself (course sellers, tool affiliates) —
  classic "people talking about making money" territory. Genuine seller
  P&L data is scarce in what surfaced.

**Verdict:** real consumer demand, but seller economics require testing
budget, content production skill, and tolerance for a loss-making startup
period Steven's constraints (limited hours, limited capital, wants fast
signal) make unattractive as a *first* test.

## Print on demand

- Generic apparel segment is saturated (15–20% margins, rising ad costs).
- Niche/differentiated products (collectibles, trading cards) report
  40–60% margins. [Yoycol](https://www.yoycol.com/blog/is-print-on-demand-profitable/)
- Success depends on brand/design differentiation — a creative and
  marketing skill set, not one AI collapses into a single prompt for a
  non-designer.

**Verdict:** real demand, but wins go to differentiated creative output,
which is not Steven's strength and not something AI reliably substitutes
for at a beginner's taste level.

## Digital products (Etsy, Gumroad)

- Confirmed sellable categories: Notion templates, AI prompt packs,
  planners, resumes, Canva templates, courses.
  [conversionproplus.com](https://conversionproplus.com/blog/gumroad-trends-2026-what-s-selling-right-now)
- Zero shipping, zero chargebacks-on-physical-goods, sold repeatedly at
  near-zero marginal cost.
- **Missing evidence:** nothing surfaced on *discovery* — how buyers find
  a specific new listing among thousands. This is the classic "build a
  product → hope someone searches for it" pattern the mission explicitly
  wants deprioritized versus demand-capture channels.
- Relevant synergy for Steven specifically: he already builds real Make.com
  scenarios. A **template/blueprint of an actual working scenario** is a
  near-zero-marginal-cost digital product built from an asset he already
  has — this is evaluated in Phase 4/9 as a secondary, not primary, move.

**Verdict:** real category demand, but acquisition is the unsolved part —
downgraded versus marketplace-native models where the buyer already
initiates contact.

## AI-assisted virtual assistant / admin services

- VA market projected to $8.6B by 2030, 22.3% CAGR.
  [Vellum](https://www.vellum.ai/blog/best-ai-assistants-for-small-business)
- 2026 demand has shifted from pure admin toward operations, lead
  management, customer support, research, and "AI-assisted workflow
  support" — squarely overlapping what Steven already does.
  [allsikes.com](https://allsikes.com/blog/from-admin-to-strategy-high-demand-virtual-assistant-services-in-2026/)
- Buyers are small businesses paying $20–$500/month for AI-assisted
  support, reporting 40–60% cost reduction vs. hiring.

**Verdict:** real and adjacent to automation work — effectively the same
buyer (a small business owner who wants less manual work) reached through
a different framing. Not a separate bet; a repositioning option.

## White-label subcontracting

- Real, large market ($99B white-label market in 2026; IT outsourcing
  headed past $1.2T by 2030).
- But the operating model *requires already having an agency relationship*
  — the cold-start problem is who introduces Steven to the agency in the
  first place. No evidence surfaced of a low-friction discovery channel
  for a first-time subcontractor with zero track record.

**Verdict:** real demand, but not solving Steven's actual current
bottleneck (getting a first buyer conversation) — it just relocates that
same problem to a harder buyer (an agency owner vetting a stranger).

## Freelance-marketplace beginner mechanics (cross-cutting evidence)

This is the single most load-bearing finding for the whole mission:

- "Your first job doesn't have to be your best-paid job — it just has to
  get you reviews. Once you have 2–3 five-star reviews, everything
  changes." [uphunt.io](https://uphunt.io/blog/how-to-get-first-client-upwork-2026-complete-guide)
- "Smaller fixed scopes ($100–$500) carry lower risk for the client, so
  they're more willing to take a chance on a freelancer with zero
  reviews." [uphunt.io](https://uphunt.io/blog/how-to-get-first-client-upwork-2026-complete-guide)
- "Working with clients who have no Upwork experience is one of the
  easiest ways to land your first client and 5-star review — they're more
  open to working with new freelancers like them." [uphunt.io](https://uphunt.io/blog/how-to-get-first-client-upwork-2026-complete-guide)

This is a near-exact description of the live opportunity already
identified this session (a first-time UK client, $150 fixed, Make.com job).
The mechanism problem was never "automation isn't a real business" — it's
that ~20 outbound touches produced 0 replies, which is a **targeting and
channel** failure, not a **model** failure. See 02 and 06 for the
comparison that proves this rather than assumes it.

## Reddit-reported reality (informal, directional only)

- Beginners in documented AI side hustles report **$500–$1,000/month in
  the first six months**; the jump to $3,000–$8,000/month takes 6–12
  months of real client work, not tool acquisition.
  [incomelab.me](https://incomelab.me/ai-side-hustles/reddit)
- The most commonly cited failure mode: the **"tool collection trap"** —
  spending $300–$500/month on tools while landing zero clients.
  [incomelab.me](https://incomelab.me/ai-side-hustles/reddit)
- This is a direct warning against Steven's demonstrated pattern
  (substantial time building assets, 20 touches, 0 replies). See
  11-STOP-DOING.md.
