# 09 — First $100 Plan

**WHO:** A first-time or low-history Upwork/Fiverr client — solo owner or
small business — who built (or attempted) a Make.com automation
themselves, often with AI assistance, and needs it finished, fixed, or
verified.

**WHAT:** A narrowly scoped fix, completion, or QA pass on an existing
Make.com scenario, delivered async with a written test summary.

**WHERE:** Upwork fixed-price listings (primary), Fiverr Make.com/n8n
category (secondary). Buyer-initiated discovery only — no outbound.

**PRICE:** $99–$150 fixed per job. The verified live opportunity (07) is
priced at exactly $150.

**HOW MANY SALES:** 1 job at $150, or 2 jobs averaging ~$50–$75 (e.g. one
Fiverr QA gig plus a smaller Upwork fix), gets past $100. A single win on
the already-identified live job clears the target in one transaction.

**HOW STEVEN GETS PAID:** Platform escrow — Upwork releases fixed-price
funds to Steven's account on milestone/job approval; Fiverr similarly
holds and releases on delivery acceptance. No separate invoicing needed
for this first milestone; the platform is the payment rail.

## The actual sequence

1. Submit the proposal already drafted for the verified live job (gated
   on reading the real task text first — see 07).
2. In parallel, run the 72-hour search recipe for 1–2 more candidates.
3. First job awarded → deliver exactly what's scoped, test hard, document
   in plain English, ask for the review explicitly but not pushily once
   delivered.
4. First $100+ banked the moment that job is approved and funds released.

## Why this is the *shortest* credible path, not just *a* path

Every other model in 05-TOP-5.md requires either a longer runway to first
signal (digital product discovery, admin retainer sourcing) or more
capital/time at risk before any revenue (ecommerce). This path has a
same-day bid, an escrow-backed payout, and — critically — a buyer who has
already typed out a real problem, removing the "does anyone actually want
this" uncertainty every other model in this file still carries.
