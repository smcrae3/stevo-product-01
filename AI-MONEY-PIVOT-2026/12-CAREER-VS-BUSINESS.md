# 12 — Career vs. Business Decision

## The three options, evaluated on expected value

**A. Aggressively pursuing this business (full focus, quit job or go all
in)** — Not supported. Zero confirmed revenue to date means there is no
track record justifying replacing a stable income stream. Expected value
of "aggressive pursuit" right now is dominated by the risk of losing
guaranteed income for an unproven, still-unvalidated channel — even one
with better evidence than before.

**B. Business as a side experiment, career primary** — Supported by the
evidence. The winning model (06) is specifically chosen because it fits
inside limited founder hours: async bidding, async delivery, no live-call
requirement in the default case. The 72-hour test (08) is designed to run
alongside a full-time job, not instead of it. This lets Steven capture
the (now better-evidenced) upside of the business without betting the
household's stable income on an unproven channel.

**C. Pausing business, focusing almost entirely on career study** — Not
supported, but not because the business is guaranteed to work. It's
because the marginal cost of the 72-hour test is near zero (a few dollars
in Connects, evening hours already being spent on this research anyway)
against a real, evidenced payoff if the channel converts. Pausing entirely
forfeits a cheap experiment for no offsetting gain — study time isn't
meaningfully increased by abandoning a test that costs a few hours a week.

## Answer: **B — treat business as a side experiment while career remains
primary**, at least until the first $1,000 is banked.

## Why this isn't just "encouragement," per the no-hype rule

The two tracks don't compete for the same hours the way "aggressive
business pursuit" vs. "study" would:

- Certification study is scheduled, deep-focus time (courses, labs).
- The winning business model's actual task list — reading a job post,
  scoring it against the instrument, writing a short proposal, testing a
  scenario, writing a plain-English summary — is short-burst, async work
  that fits in the same kind of pockets of time (lunch break, evening
  hour) without requiring the same deep-focus block a cert module does.

This is why option B is chosen over a forced trade-off between the two:
the evidence doesn't show they're actually competing for the same
resource in the way options A and C assume.

## Re-evaluation trigger

Revisit this decision only when one of two things happens:
1. **Business signal is strong and repeated** — multiple 72-hour windows
   (08) clear the success threshold and $1,000 is banked — at which point
   B could shift toward A with real evidence behind it, not hope.
2. **Business signal is genuinely absent** — repeated 72-hour windows hit
   the failure threshold despite correct scoring and bidding — at which
   point hours currently split toward the business shift back fully to C.

Until one of those triggers fires, B stands.
