# 05 — Distribution Test

## Where are the buyers, and does Steven chase or get found?

| Lane | Buyer posts need? | Steven searches marketplace? | Already belongs to an agency (Steven rides along)? | Steven chasing strangers? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Automation repair | **YES** — buyer posts a job describing a broken/DIY scenario | YES — Steven searches and bids | No | **No**, once bidding replaces outbound |
| B — Subcontracting | No accessible posting mechanism found | N/A — no marketplace surfaced | Requires Steven to already have the relationship — **not found as a discoverable channel** | **Yes, functionally** — the "front door" itself has to be chased |
| C — Nontech AI services | **YES** — buyer posts a job (VA, research, cleanup) | YES — Steven searches and bids | No | **No** |

## Reward built-in demand capture, per instruction

Lanes A and C both have the buyer-posts / Steven-responds structure the
mission explicitly wants rewarded. Lane B fails this test outright — not
on demand (real) but on **discoverability of the door in**. This is the
single clearest differentiator among the three lanes and the main reason
B is eliminated before the final scoring in 08-WINNER.md, regardless of
how the automation-lane correction affects A.

## A vs. C on distribution specifically

Both pass the structural test equally. The difference is downstream, not
in *whether* the buyer initiates: A's category has stronger individually-
traceable evidence (real indexed job titles were found; none were found
for C), while C's category has a lower price floor from global
competition. Distribution access is a tie; economics and evidence
strength are not (see 08-WINNER.md).
