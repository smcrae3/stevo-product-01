# 02 — Lane B: Subcontracting / White-Label Delivery — Live Demand

## What was actually found

Every search this turn aimed at finding an accessible entry point — "junior
automation specialist," "automation delivery assistant," "implementation
support," "white-label automation subcontractor hiring" — returned one of
two things, neither of which is a zero-review-beginner-accessible gig:

1. **Agency-side strategy content**, written *for* agency owners deciding
   whether to use white-label partners, not job postings from agencies
   looking for junior implementers. ("The conversation inside most digital
   agencies in 2026 has shifted from should we offer AI services? to how
   do we deliver them at scale.") This tells us agencies exist and are
   growing — it does not show a discoverable channel for Steven to reach
   one.
2. **Salaried/experienced-hire job boards** (ZipRecruiter): "AI Automation
   Agency" roles averaging **$91,500–$139,000/year**, and "Automation
   Specialist" roles averaging **$57,000–$98,500/year**. These are
   full-time or serious-contractor roles that assume real prior experience
   — not a bounded, zero-review, AI-assisted entry point.

**No real posting was found of the shape "agency looking for a junior/AI-
assisted implementer to handle bounded delivery tasks, apply here."**

## Why this matters for the distribution test

Lane B's entire theoretical appeal (per the mission's own framing) is that
Steven wouldn't have to own sales/discovery — the agency already has the
client. But that only helps if there's a **discoverable front door** into
an agency's subcontractor network. The evidence found does not show one:
no beginner-facing job board, no marketplace, no "apply to become an
implementation partner" page surfaced anywhere in five targeted searches.

The likely real channel — a warm referral or a direct relationship with an
agency owner — is not a demand-capture channel at all. It's back to
outbound relationship-building, the exact acquisition mode the mission
(and Steven) explicitly wants to avoid as the *core* strategy.

## Beginner-safe filter

Even where roles were found (documentation, workflow QA, implementation
support keywords), the postings that surfaced skew toward requirements
implying prior professional experience (résumé/portfolio screening,
established skill claims) rather than a bounded, single-task, text-first
gig a zero-review beginner could credibly win same-day.

## Verdict for Lane B

**Real demand exists at the market level** (the white-label industry
figure — $99B market in 2026 — is real), **but no accessible, beginner-
facing entry point was found.** This is a repeat and reinforcement of the
same conclusion from the prior report (02-BUSINESS-MODEL-RANKING.md row 3,
scored 20/100 on Steven Fit / P(first $100)): the cold-start problem simply
relocates from "find a buyer" to "find an agency willing to vet a
stranger," and no evidence surfaced this turn that this door is any more
open than it was last time.
