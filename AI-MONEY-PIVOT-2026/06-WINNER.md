# 06 — The Winner

## WINNER: Marketplace-native Make.com fix/finish/QA gigs (narrow scope)

No ties, as instructed. This is **not** a change of business model —
it's a narrowing of the one Steven already has, chosen because the
evidence supports it better than every alternative tested, not because
it's the incumbent.

## The direct answer the mission demands

**If the correct conclusion is still automation freelancing: say so and
prove why.**

It is. Here's the proof, not an assertion:

1. Every business model in 02-BUSINESS-MODEL-RANKING.md was scored on the
   same 12 axes with no thumb on the scale for the incumbent. Rows 1, 2,
   and 14 (the automation/services cluster) won on the composite, and row
   2 — narrow troubleshooting specifically — won outright.
2. Ecommerce (03) was evaluated seriously and rejected on evidence
   (beginner net margins run negative in the first 90 days; the modern
   TikTok Shop variant is real but doesn't remove that capital-risk
   problem).
3. Digital products (04, 05 #4) were kept as a real, evidenced category —
   but downgraded because no evidence exists for how a new listing gets
   *found*, which fails Phase 5's core preference for demand-capture
   channels.
4. Subcontracting and chatbot builds were rejected not on demand (both
   real) but on Steven Fit — subcontracting has the same cold-start
   problem relocated to a harder buyer, and chatbot builds sit past the
   line in the Phase 7 understanding test.

## What actually changes versus what Steven has been doing

**Not the model. The scope and the target.**

Twenty qualified outbound touches with zero replies is not evidence that
automation freelancing doesn't work — the market evidence (Phase 1) shows
this category transacting daily at exactly Steven's price point. It's
evidence that **outbound cold-touch is the wrong channel** for a
zero-review freelancer. The fix proven by the market's own advice
("smaller fixed scopes carry lower risk for the client," "first-time
clients are the easiest first win," "your first job's job is to get you
reviews") is:

- **Stop:** broad outbound touches to strangers who didn't ask.
- **Start:** narrow, same-day bids on marketplace-posted jobs where the
  buyer already typed out their problem, filtered through the hard
  rejection filter and understanding test already built in
  `CLAUDE-CLOSER/LATEST-CLOSER-DECISION.md`.

This is a real pivot in *channel and scope discipline*, even though it
keeps the underlying skill set. It is not "protecting the current
business model" — it is the model surviving a fair fight against
ecommerce, digital products, subcontracting, and services adjacency, and
winning on evidence.
