## HIGHEST-VALUE SEARCH DIRECTION FOR ANTIGRAVITY

One direction, not a list:

> **Upwork jobs where an existing Make.com scenario is already broken or half-finished,
> posted by a non-technical business owner, budget $75–$250, fixed price.**

Why this direction and not another:

- The buyer has already bought the tool. Demand is proven, not hypothesized.
- A broken scenario is a bounded problem with a visible finish line — it works or it
  doesn't. Scope cannot quietly expand the way "build me an automation" does.
- Make's UI is visual. Steven can look at the failing module and describe what he sees
  in plain English without knowing the API underneath.
- Non-technical posters communicate by text and don't run live technical interrogations.
- Small budget means few proposals from agencies, and a 5-star review on a $99 job is
  worth the same on the profile as a 5-star on a $2,000 one.

Search shape: Make.com / Integromat + words like *broken, not working, error, fix,
finish, stopped working, help*. Exclude anything naming n8n, Twilio, Vapi, Retell,
ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, MLS, or a CRM API.

## SCORING INSTRUMENT (apply to each candidate on arrival)

Score 0–100 each. Any single score under 40 kills the candidate.

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| Technical fit | Is this Make / Sheets / Forms / Gmail / simple routing? |
| Steven understanding | Can Steven follow what the system is supposed to do? |
| AI-assistability | Can AI safely carry the implementation? |
| Text-first friendliness | Can this close and deliver over messages? |
| Scope clarity | Is "done" defined in the post itself? |
| Delivery confidence | Odds of shipping something genuinely good |
| First-review risk | Odds this buyer leaves a bad review even if delivery is fine |
| Commercial intent | Payment verified, hire history, replies to proposals |
| Competition | Proposal count and who else is bidding |
| Price / economics | Is the money worth the hours and the risk? |

### The gating test

**CAN STEVEN EXPLAIN THIS JOB TO THE CUSTOMER WITHOUT HIDING BEHIND AI?**

`YES` / `PARTLY` / `NO` — **NO = SKIP, regardless of every other score.**

`PARTLY` is allowed only when the unclear part is a single named module Steven can go
open and look at before replying.

### Hard rejection filter (automatic skip, no scoring)

deep APIs · MLS or real-estate valuation logic · advanced Apps Script · deep JavaScript ·
deep n8n · Twilio / Vapi / Retell · advanced CRM systems · ServiceTitan / Housecall Pro
internals · complex authentication · enterprise systems · cybersecurity · SaaS
engineering · large databases · specialist mobile or web development · anything Steven
cannot restate in plain English.

## CALL CHEAT SHEET (blank — fill per candidate)

Text-first is the default. This exists only for when a buyer insists on a call.

**WHAT THE CLIENT WANTS**
> One sentence, in the client's own words.

**WHAT STEVEN CAN DO**
> Only what has actually been done before or is plainly within Make / Sheets / Gmail.

**WHAT STEVEN CANNOT DO**
> Name it out loud before the client discovers it.

**5 QUESTIONS TO ASK**
1. Walk me through what happens today, from the customer's first contact.
2. Show me the scenario — can you share a screenshot of the error?
3. When did it last work, and what changed around then?
4. What does "fixed" look like to you?
5. Who else touches this setup?

**3 THINGS NOT TO PROMISE**
1. A timeline before seeing the actual scenario.
2. Anything involving a system not yet looked at.
3. Ongoing maintenance or support beyond the fix.

**WHAT TO SAY IF STEVEN DOESN'T KNOW**
> "I don't want to guess on that. Let me look at the actual workflow/error first and I'll
> tell you whether it's something I can handle."

Second safe phrase, if pushed:
> "That might be outside what I do. If it is, I'll tell you straight rather than take the job."

No bluffing. Saying "that's outside what I do" loses one job. Guessing wrong loses the
first review.
