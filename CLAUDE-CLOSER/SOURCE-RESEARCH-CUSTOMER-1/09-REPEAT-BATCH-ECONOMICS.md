# 09 — Repeat-Batch Economics (Future Planning Only)

**This does not renegotiate the $50 / 20-utility trial.** That price is
fixed as posted. Everything below is planning for a *future* batch
conversation, only relevant after the trial proves quality.

## The core fact: hourly rate depends only on minutes/row, not batch size

At a flat $2.50/row, a bigger batch does not improve the hourly rate —
it just repeats the same rate more times. This matters because "bigger
batch" can *feel* like better economics when it isn't automatically one.

| Minutes/row | Records/hour | Hourly value at $2.50/row | 50-record batch | 100-record batch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 min | 12 | $30.00/hr | 4.2 hrs → $125 | 8.3 hrs → $250 |
| 8 min | 7.5 | $18.75/hr | 6.7 hrs → $125 | 13.3 hrs → $250 |
| 10 min | 6 | $15.00/hr | 8.3 hrs → $125 | 16.7 hrs → $250 |
| 15 min | 4 | $10.00/hr | 12.5 hrs → $125 | 25.0 hrs → $250 |

## What the actual 20-row trial will tell us

The delivery simulation for the trial batch estimated a realistic average
around **11–12 minutes/row** (mixing easy, normal, and difficult records
with the time-box caps applied). At that average, $2.50/row lands close to
**$13–15/hr** — workable, but not comfortably above the $15/hr threshold
most freelancers would consider a solid floor for this kind of work.

**At what average minutes/row does $2.50/row clearly become worthwhile?**
Roughly **≤8 minutes/row** to comfortably clear $18–19/hr. Above ~12
minutes/row, $2.50/row starts to look thin rather than sustainable.

## Recommendation for repeat batches

**Do not assume $2.50/row is automatically the right ongoing price.**
Use the trial batch's own actual, measured average minutes/row (tracked
plainly — start/stop timing per record, or at minimum an honest post-hoc
estimate) as the real evidence, not a guess made now.

- If the trial's actual average lands at or under ~8 min/row: $2.50/row is
  reasonable to continue for the next batch.
- If the trial's actual average lands materially higher (10–15+ min/row,
  which the delivery simulation suggests is plausible given how many tiny
  towns are likely to appear): propose **$3–$4/row** for the next
  negotiated batch, citing the trial's real time data as the reason, not
  an arbitrary increase.
- **$5/row** is worth testing only if a future batch skews toward
  harder records specifically (e.g., a state with unusually many
  sub-1,000-population systems) — not as a default next step.

The conversation to have with the client, when it comes up, is straight
forward and evidence-based: "Here's roughly how long the trial actually
took me per record — does $X/row work for the next batch at that pace?"
— not a unilateral price change presented after the fact.
