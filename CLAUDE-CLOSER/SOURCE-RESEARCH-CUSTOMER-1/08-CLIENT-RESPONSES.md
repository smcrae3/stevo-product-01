# 08 — Client Communication

All responses truthful, none promising something the process can't back up.

## A. "How long will 20 records take?"

> Most of them go quickly — a clear staff directory or department page
> resolves them in a few minutes. A handful of smaller towns take longer
> since I check county and state-level sources before giving up on a
> named contact. I'd expect the batch done within a day of starting it.

## B. "Can you guarantee every record has an email?"

> No — some smaller utilities just don't publish an individual email
> anywhere I can verify. For those I'll give you the best verified
> alternative (a contact form link or general office email) and note
> that no individual email was publicly available, rather than guess one
> that might bounce or be wrong.

## C. "Why is this one blank?"

> That means I checked the sources I normally would — [name the specific
> ones actually checked for that row] — and didn't find anything I could
> verify as accurate. I'd rather leave it blank than put in something
> that looks right but isn't. The notes column on that row says exactly
> what I checked.

## D. "Can you guess the likely email?"

> I'd rather not — a guessed email that's wrong is worse for you than a
> blank one, since it can look legitimate and end up bouncing or going to
> the wrong person. If you'd like, I can give you the general office
> contact or contact form instead, which I know is a real, working
> channel.

## E. "Can you do another 100?"

> Yes, happy to. Since this first batch is the trial, I'd want to run it
> to completion first so you can see the actual quality and judgment
> calls before we agree on the next batch's scope and price.

(Per instruction: this response does not renegotiate the first $50 trial,
and it doesn't quote a number for the next batch — that's deliberately
left open until quality is proven and priced properly; see
`09-REPEAT-BATCH-ECONOMICS.md`.)
