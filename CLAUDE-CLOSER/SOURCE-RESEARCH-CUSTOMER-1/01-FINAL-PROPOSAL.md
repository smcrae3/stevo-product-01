## The proposal

> Hi — I'd like to help with this. My approach for each utility:
>
> I start with the official city, utility, or district website — the water
> or public works department page and staff directory first. If the
> person overseeing plant operations and a published email aren't there,
> I check the county government site, the state's Rural Water Association
> directory, and state drinking-water/wastewater program records. I use
> LinkedIn only to confirm a name and title, never as a source for an
> email.
>
> I never construct or guess an email address. If one isn't publicly
> published, I leave that field blank and supply the contact form URL or
> general office email instead. Every filled field gets the exact source
> link next to it. If a plant is closed, outsourced, or merged, I note
> that rather than force a contact that isn't real.
>
> I haven't completed a paid utility-directory project before, but this
> kind of careful, source-by-source public research — and being honest
> about what I can't verify — is exactly the work I do well.
>
> $50 for the 20-utility batch works for me as posted.

## Why it's built this way

- **Opens with method, not a credential claim** — the client's own hard
  rules (never guess, always source) are answered before they're even
  asked, which does more to earn trust than a resume line would.
- **States the experience gap in one plain sentence, once** — no
  apologizing, no padding around it, no implying something that isn't
  true.
- **Names the actual fallback chain** (county → state RWA → state
  drinking-water records → LinkedIn for name/title only) so the client
  can see the judgment before hiring, not after.
- **Confirms the price as posted** — no discount offered, no upsell
  attempted.
