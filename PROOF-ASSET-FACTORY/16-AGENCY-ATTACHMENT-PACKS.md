# 16 — AGENCY PITCH ATTACHMENT PACKS

**Rule: never attach more than two assets to cold outreach.** A reviewer
evaluating a stranger's cold email spends seconds, not minutes, deciding
whether to open an attachment. Two well-matched samples say "this person
understood what I need." Six samples say "this person is hoping something
sticks" — and it forces the reviewer to do matching work Steven should have
already done for them. Every pack below is capped at 2.

---

## PACK A — DOCUMENTATION AGENCY
*(VA agencies, ops-support agencies, agencies whose core service is
process/documentation work)*

**Assets:** SOP / Process Documentation (`01` + `02`, sent as one linked
document) + Requirements Brief (`07`)

**Why these two:** The SOP is the single clearest demonstration of the core
skill this buyer type hires for. The Requirements Brief adds a second
dimension — spreadsheet/data-operations thinking — that's relevant to the
generalist operations work these agencies typically place people into,
without repeating the same skill twice.

**Why not more:** Adding the QA pack or CX analysis here would dilute the
"documentation specialist" positioning into "generalist with five different
samples," which is a weaker first impression for this specific buyer type.

---

## PACK B — AUTOMATION AGENCY
*(Make.com/Zapier/n8n builders, no-code agencies)*

**Assets:** QA / Workflow Testing pack (`04` + `05`, sent as one linked
document, summary table + 2-3 representative bug reports rather than the
full 38-case pack in the initial email) + SOP / Process Documentation (`01`)

**Why these two:** The QA pack is the exact bounded, low-liability offer
(pre-delivery QA) that's easiest for this buyer to say yes to first — it
demonstrates the skill directly. The SOP adds the handoff-documentation
angle, which is the natural second sale once QA trust is established (per
the prior mission's Offer 1 → Offer 2 ladder).

**Why not more:** The Requirements Brief risks reading as "trying to do the
agency's own job" (requirements gathering is typically their internal
strength) rather than filling a gap — better held in reserve for a
follow-up conversation if they ask what else Steven does.

---

## PACK C — OPERATIONS / CX AGENCY
*(CX consultancies, customer-experience-focused firms, retention/support
operations shops)*

**Assets:** CX Operations Analysis (`08`) + SOP / Process Documentation
(`01`)

**Why these two:** The CX analysis is Steven's most credible piece for this
buyer — it's the one place his real professional background (not AI
assistance) is the substance, and it directly demonstrates the analytical
output this buyer type sells to their own clients. The SOP shows he can
also formalize the fixes his analysis recommends, closing the loop from
"found the problem" to "wrote the procedure that prevents it."

**Why not more:** The QA pack and Requirements Brief are both too far from
this buyer's domain to strengthen the pitch — including them risks reading
as generic rather than targeted.

---

## THE UNDERLYING LOGIC

Every pack pairs **one asset that directly matches the buyer's core service**
with **one asset that shows a natural adjacent capability** — never two
assets demonstrating the same skill, and never an asset that's simply
irrelevant to that buyer type. This mirrors the prior mission's agency
offer ladder: lead with the easiest yes, follow with the natural next step,
and hold everything else back for when the relationship has already earned
a second look.

**One more attachment rule that applies to all three packs:** if a specific
agency's own public work signals a particular gap (e.g., their case studies
show builds but never mention testing), lead with whichever pack asset
matches that specific observed gap — the "specific detail" personalization
requirement from the prior mission's outreach rules applies to asset
selection too, not just the message text.
