## BEFORE — WHAT THE CLIENT ACTUALLY HANDS OVER

*(Full raw material is in `18-SOP-RAW-INPUT.md` — reproduced in condensed
form here for the direct before/after contrast.)*

A rambling voice-note transcript ("um, how we handle the — when someone
emails us..."), a screenshot of a group-chat conversation full of
unresolved questions ("should billing go to me? ... yeah probably? unless
its like a really quick one"), and a bullet list titled "process stuff ??"
that raises more questions than it answers.

**Total raw material: ~600 words across three disorganized sources, with
at least one unresolved internal contradiction and zero concrete
definitions.**

---

## AFTER — WHAT COMES BACK

A structured, 18-section SOP (`01-SOP-DEMONSTRATION.md`) with document
control, defined roles, a concrete urgency-classification table with
example signals, a step-by-step procedure, an exceptions section that
specifically resolves the duplicate-contact failure mentioned in the raw
material, escalation rules, a quality-check schedule, and a one-page quick
reference — plus a companion process map (`02-SOP-PROCESS-MAP.md`) showing
the entire flow, including exception and escalation paths, as one
connected diagram.

**Total finished material: a complete operational document a new hire could
follow on day one, with every ambiguity from the raw material either
resolved (via the clarifying questions in `18`) or explicitly flagged as an
example policy pending owner confirmation.**

## THE SPECIFIC TRANSFORMATIONS — traced directly, not just claimed

| Raw material said... | The SOP resolved it as... |
|---|---|
| "whoever sees it first just kind of deals with it" | A defined subject-area routing table with a named fallback (Operations Lead) for anything unmatched |
| "sometimes people say urgent and it's not... sometimes people don't say urgent and it actually is" | A three-tier urgency table with concrete example signals for each level, plus an explicit rule: when ambiguous, round up rather than down |
| The three-emails-nobody-noticed story | A specific duplicate-detection exception (Case B) requiring records to be checked by customer + summary similarity within 24 hours before creating a new entry |

| No mention anywhere of what happens if nobody responds after resolution | The SOP adds an explicit 3-business-day auto-close rule — a gap the raw material never surfaced but that any real deployment needs answered |

## WHAT THIS PROVES ABOUT THE 48-HOUR OFFER

This demonstrates the actual mechanics of the productized service: the
client hands over exactly the kind of disorganized, contradictory,
incomplete material real business owners actually produce (not a clean
brief), and the deliverable both **resolves what can be resolved from the
material itself** and **explicitly asks about what can't be** — rather than
either guessing silently or refusing to proceed without a perfect brief.

**The clarifying-questions step is not a delay tactic — it's included in
the 48-hour turnaround.** A real engagement would send these six questions
back within the first few hours, get quick answers (most are single-
sentence answers for the client), and proceed. The finished SOP already
reflects reasonable resolutions where a quick answer is expected to
confirm rather than change the direction (e.g., "acknowledge even if you
can't fix it same day" — reasonably assumed as a very likely yes based on
Source 3's own "probably yes," but the finished SOP would flag this exact
assumption for confirmation on a real job rather than treating it as
already settled).
