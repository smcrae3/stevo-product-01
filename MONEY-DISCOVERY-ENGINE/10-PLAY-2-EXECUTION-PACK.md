# 10 — PLAY 2 EXECUTION PACK: AGENCY BACK-END PRODUCTION PARTNER

## POSITIONING
The person an agency owner hands the unglamorous delivery work to and stops
thinking about. Not a competitor. Not a junior automation builder. **Capacity
with judgment.**

## ONE-SENTENCE OFFER
> I write your client handover documentation and QA your builds before
> delivery, white-labeled, on 48-72 hour turnaround — so your team stays on
> billable build work.

## BUYER PROFILE
- 2-15 person automation / marketing / ops agencies, or solo operators with
  more clients than hours.
- Signals they're a fit: hiring or "looking for help" posts; complaining
  about scope, docs, or delivery load; shipping builds fast; visible client
  roster.
- **Decision maker: the owner.** One conversation, no procurement.

## QUALIFICATION RULES — take the job if
- Scope is **bounded and named** (a deliverable, not "help with stuff").
- Rate ≥ $20/hr or ≥ $150 for a bounded unit.
- The agency, not Steven, owns the client relationship and the liability.
- Steven can inspect and defend every artifact he hands over.

**Walk away if:** they want free "test" work beyond ~30 minutes; they want
Steven client-facing under their brand without agreeing terms; they want
production system access with no supervision; they push fixed price on
open-ended scope; payment terms exceed net-30 on a first engagement.

## PRICING
| Deliverable | Price |
|---|---|
| Client handover doc / SOP set | **$25-40/hr**, or $200-400 per build documented |
| Pre-delivery QA pass | **$20-35/hr**, or $150-300 per build |
| Research pack | **$20-35/hr**, or $150-400 per pack |
| Build-assist (Make/Sheets, supervised) | **$25-40/hr — hourly only** |

**First engagement: a paid trial at $150-300 on a real, small piece of work.**
Not free. Free work attracts agencies that never convert and signals that
Steven's time has no price.

## DELIVERY SCOPE
Documentation, SOPs, QA passes and bug sheets, research packs, structured
data prep, workflow write-ups — all white-labeled to the agency's brand and
template.

## OUT OF SCOPE — say this explicitly
- Direct client contact unless the agency explicitly arranges it.
- Owning production systems, credentials, or on-call fixes.
- Anything Steven can't inspect or explain — **including AI output he hasn't
  personally verified.**
- Unsupervised architecture decisions on live client infrastructure.
- n8n, CRM implementation, security work.

## DELIVERY SOP
1. **Intake (15-30 min call).** Who's the end client? What's the deliverable?
   What template/brand? What's "done"? What's the deadline? Any access needed?
2. **Restate scope in writing** before starting. One paragraph. Get a yes.
   This single step prevents most scope disputes.
3. **Gather source material** — recordings, existing docs, the build itself.
4. **AI first draft.** Never the deliverable — the raw material.
5. **Steven's verification pass.** Every claim, step, and screenshot checked
   against the actual system. This pass is what he's paid for.
6. **Format to their template**, their voice, their brand.
7. **Self-QA** against the checklist below.
8. **Deliver early**, with a 3-line summary: what's included, what's not,
   what he'd flag.
9. **Ask one question** that shows he understood their business. This is how
   a one-off becomes a retainer.

## QUALITY CHECKLIST
- [ ] Would their client understand this without asking a follow-up?
- [ ] Did I verify every step against the real system, or just trust the AI?
- [ ] Matches their template, voice, and branding exactly?
- [ ] Any placeholder, TODO, or hallucinated detail left in? (Search the doc.)
- [ ] Are screenshots current and unredacted-sensitive?
- [ ] Did I flag what I couldn't verify rather than paper over it?
- [ ] Delivered before the deadline, not on it?

## SAMPLE / PROOF PLAN — build this before any outreach (~3 hrs)
1. **Sample SOP (2-3 pages).** Document a real process Steven actually
   knows — e.g. "Google Form → Sheet → automated confirmation email: setup
   and maintenance." Steps, owners, screenshots, failure modes, what to do
   when it breaks.
2. **Sample QA bug sheet.** Take any real workflow, test it deliberately,
   log findings: issue / severity / repro steps / expected vs actual /
   suggested fix.

These two artifacts unlock **both Play 2 and Play 3.** Highest-leverage 3
hours in the entire plan.

## AGENCY SUBCONTRACT PITCH — the 3 lines for a call
1. "You have more delivery work than delivery hours. I'm not trying to sell
   you strategy — I take bounded production off your plate."
2. "Documentation and QA specifically, because that's where agencies bleed
   retention and it's the last thing anyone wants to do at 6pm Friday."
3. "Start with one paid piece. If the quality isn't there, you've lost $200
   and a Tuesday."

## CLIENT INTAKE QUESTIONS
1. What exactly is the deliverable, and who reads it?
2. What does "done" look like — can you show me a past one you liked?
3. Template, brand voice, format?
4. What access do I need, and what should I *not* touch?
5. Deadline — and what's the real one behind the stated one?
6. Who reviews my work before it reaches the client?
7. If this goes well, what's the next piece?
8. Payment terms and schedule?

## OBJECTION RESPONSES
**"We already have someone."** → "Understood — capacity partners are usually
about overflow, not replacement. Keep my samples; when a deadline compresses,
I turn around a handover doc in 48 hours."

**"How do I know your quality?"** → "You don't yet. That's what the samples
and a $200 first piece are for. I'd rather earn the second job than argue
for the first."

**"You're not technical enough."** → *Do not oversell here.* "That's fair —
I'm not your build engineer and I won't pretend to be. What I'm good at is
taking a build someone else made and making it explainable, tested, and
documented. That's a different skill and it's the one you're short on."

**"Can you do the actual automation work?"** → "Simple Make.com and Sheets
work, hourly, under your review — yes. Architecture or anything I can't
fully debug myself — no, and I'll tell you before I take it, not after."

**"What's your rate?"** → "$25-40 an hour depending on the work, or fixed
per deliverable if the scope is clearly bounded. For the first piece I'd
suggest a fixed $200 so there's no risk to you."

## SUCCESS / FAILURE CRITERIA
- **Week 1:** 2 samples built; 10 researched messages sent.
- **Week 2:** 2+ replies; 1+ call booked. *(If zero replies from 10 → the
  targeting or the message is wrong, not the offer. Rewrite, don't scale.)*
- **Week 4:** 1 paid trial delivered.
- **Week 8:** 1 agency sending repeat work.
- **Kill signal:** 40 well-researched messages, zero calls → the offer is
  wrong for this market. Reassess rather than sending 40 more.
