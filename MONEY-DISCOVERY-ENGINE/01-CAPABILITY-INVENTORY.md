# 01 — CAPABILITY INVENTORY

**Basis:** Steven's self-report in the mission brief. The Windows workspace
was unreachable (see `00-READ-THIS-FIRST-WORKSPACE-GAP.md`), so nothing
below is verified against an actual artifact. Items marked `[UNVERIFIED]`
must not be claimed to a buyer until Steven opens the file and confirms.

## Grading key

- **A — PROVEN.** Done successfully, unaided, repeatably. Sellable today.
- **B — PROVEN WITH AI ASSISTANCE.** Output existed, but AI did substantial
  technical lifting. Sellable *if* Steven can inspect and defend the output.
- **C — NEAR-TERM.** Deliverable in 1-3 weeks with modest learning + AI.
- **D — TOO ADVANCED.** Do not sell. Do not imply.

The honest discriminator between B and A: **could Steven debug it live on a
screen-share with the client watching?** If no, it is B, and B is dangerous
to sell at a fixed price.

## THE HONEST SUMMARY

Steven has **one A-grade commercial cluster** and it is not technical:

> **Read something, judge whether it is right, explain clearly why, and
> organize the answer so a normal person can act on it.**

Communication + QA + research + structure + customer sense. That cluster is
real, unaided, and — critically — it is exactly what the AI-evaluation
industry is buying at scale in 2026, and exactly what agencies need in
back-end production.

His **technical cluster is all grade B** and every B is AI-dependent. B-grade
skills are safe to sell **hourly**, on **bounded scope**, to a buyer who
knows a human is learning on their dollar. They are **not** safe to sell as
fixed-price "I will build you an automation."

**The strategic error to avoid:** building the business around the B-grade
technical cluster (because it feels more impressive and more "AI business")
while leaving the A-grade judgment cluster unmonetized. That is backwards.
Sell the A. Let the B grow on hourly contracts where the clock protects him.
