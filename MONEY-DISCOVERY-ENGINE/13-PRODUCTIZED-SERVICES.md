# PART A — PHASE 12: WHERE AI IS A REAL PRODUCTION ADVANTAGE

**The test, applied strictly.** A task qualifies only if all four hold:
1. A competent human normally takes **5-10 hours**.
2. Steven + AI can do it in **1-3 hours**.
3. The final result is **inspectable** — a buyer can verify quality by
   looking at it.
4. **Buyers actually pay for it** (not merely "would find it useful").

Criterion 4 kills most candidates. Criterion 3 is what keeps Steven safe:
if the buyer can inspect it, Steven can also inspect it before shipping,
which is the only defense against AI error.

**Independent confirmation this arbitrage is real:** 2026 market commentary
on the VA sector notes that VAs fluent in AI tools "do in two hours what
used to take eight." Steven's edge is a documented market phenomenon, not
a hope.

## THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

AI compresses **reading, synthesizing, structuring, and drafting**.
AI does **not** reliably compress **building, debugging, and designing** —
because when AI-generated code is wrong, finding out why takes longer than
writing it, and Steven's grade-B skills can't shortcut that.

> **Sell where AI compresses. Charge hourly where it doesn't.**

This single rule explains every recommendation in this report: the top plays
are all reading/judging/structuring work, and every building task is either
killed or restricted to hourly.

## THE ONE NON-NEGOTIABLE
Steven must **personally verify** every AI-produced claim before delivery.
The inspectability that makes these services safe also makes an unverified
hallucination immediately visible to the buyer. One fabricated citation in a
research pack destroys a client relationship permanently. His verification
pass is not overhead — **it is the product.**

## PRICING RULES
1. **Never below $75.** Below that, intake and comms eat the margin entirely.
2. **One named problem per fixed price.** "Fix my sheet" is not a scope;
   "the pivot on tab 3 breaks when new rows are added" is.
3. **One revision round included, stated up front.** Further rounds hourly.
4. **Scope creep gets a new quote, cheerfully and immediately.** Not a
   confrontation — "happy to, that's a separate $X, want me to add it?"
5. Price the **outcome**, not the hours — but model the hours privately
   before quoting.

## THE HONEST CAVEAT
These are excellent **delivery** economics and they are only half the
equation. Per `06`, a $150 job at 2 hours is $75/hr — until you add 10
proposals to win it, at which point it's $24/hr, and at Steven's 27 Connects
he can't even buy the 10 proposals.

> **Productized services are a delivery format, not an acquisition strategy.**
> Run them through Play 2 (agencies), a warm contact, or a local buyer.
> Do not run them cold through Upwork at 27 Connects and expect them to work.
