# 09 — PLAY 1 EXECUTION PACK: AI EVALUATION CONTRACTOR

## POSITIONING
A careful, articulate generalist evaluator who writes critiques a reviewer
can audit — not a fast clicker. Consistency and defensible reasoning are the
product.

## ONE-SENTENCE OFFER
> I evaluate and critique AI model output with the same rigor I used running
> customer-service quality reviews: consistent to the rubric, clearly
> reasoned, and auditable.

## BUYER PROFILE
AI data vendors staffing frontier-lab projects: **Mercor, Surge AI, Outlier,
Handshake AI, Alignerr**. They are supply-constrained on careful generalists
who can *write*, and they screen hard for consistency.

## QUALIFICATION RULES (Steven's, applied to platforms)
Take a project if: rate ≥ $17/hr, US-eligible, rate is stated before work,
payment terms are published, and onboarding is ≤ 2 unpaid hours.
**Walk away** if: any fee is requested from Steven, rate is undisclosed
until after unpaid work, or payment terms are vague. **No legitimate
platform in this market charges a worker to start.**

## PRICING
Accept posted rates at entry. Do not negotiate on project #1 — leverage
comes from a quality record, and specialist/reviewer tiers ($30-55/hr on
RLHF work) are reached by *scoring well*, not by asking.

## DELIVERY SCOPE
Rating and ranking responses; written critiques; factuality and source
verification; instruction-following judgments; edge-case and safety flags.

## OUT OF SCOPE — hard no
Anything requiring credentials Steven lacks (medical, legal, financial,
senior-engineering evaluation). The specialist tiers pay $85-160/hr
precisely because they verify credentials. **Do not misrepresent
qualifications to reach them.** Getting caught ends every platform at once.

## DELIVERY SOP
1. **Read the rubric twice** before the first item. Most failures are rubric
   misreads, not judgment failures.
2. Do 3 slow calibration items. Re-read the rubric. Compare.
3. Work in **45-minute blocks**. Fatigue destroys consistency, and
   consistency is the metric they score.
4. For each item: form the judgment → *then* write the reason. Never write
   the reason first and reverse-engineer the score.
5. Critique format: **what's wrong → why it matters → what better looks
   like.** Specific, not "this response is bad."
6. Flag genuine ambiguity rather than guessing. Reviewers reward calibration.
7. Log hours honestly. Overclaiming is the fastest way to be removed.

## QUALITY CHECKLIST (before submitting a batch)
- [ ] Did I apply the same standard to item 1 and item 50?
- [ ] Would a reviewer reading only my critique understand my score?
- [ ] Did I cite the specific part of the response I'm criticizing?
- [ ] Did I check claimed facts rather than assume?
- [ ] Did I follow *their* rubric, not my preference?
- [ ] Any item I'd score differently if I saw it again? → revisit it.

## SAMPLE / PROOF PLAN
**None required.** This is the play's defining advantage. The only proof is
the assessment itself. Prepare by practicing: take three ChatGPT/Claude
answers to the same prompt, rank them, and write 100-word critiques. Thirty
minutes of this before the first assessment is the highest-ROI prep
available.

## APPLICATION MESSAGE (for profile/bio fields)
> I spent years in customer service and retention, where my job was to
> review interactions for quality, judge whether a response actually solved
> the customer's problem, and explain clearly why it did or didn't. That is
> the same skill AI evaluation requires: applying a rubric consistently,
> catching what's subtly wrong, and writing a reason someone else can check.
> I work carefully rather than quickly, I flag ambiguity instead of guessing,
> and I'm comfortable being audited.

## INTAKE QUESTIONS (ask the platform)
1. What is the rate for this project, and is onboarding paid?
2. What is the expected weekly volume, and how stable has it been?
3. How is quality scored, and how do I see my score?
4. What is the payment schedule and first payout timing?
5. What are the paths to higher-paying tiers?

*(Q2 matters most. A $30/hr project at 4 hrs/week is worse than $20/hr at 20.)*

## OBJECTION RESPONSES
**"No AI/ML background?"** → "Evaluation isn't model building — it's quality
judgment against a rubric. That's what I did professionally in CS quality
review. I know what I don't know, and I flag it rather than guess."

**"This seems too good / is it a scam?"** → Legitimacy test: they pay *you*,
rate is disclosed upfront, terms are published, no fee to start. Mercor,
Surge, Outlier and Handshake all meet this. Anything that fails it is not
this market.

**"Isn't this replaced by AI soon?"** → Partly, at the low end. Which is why
Steven targets the *written critique* tiers, not click-annotation — and why
this is a cash floor funding Play 2, not a career.
