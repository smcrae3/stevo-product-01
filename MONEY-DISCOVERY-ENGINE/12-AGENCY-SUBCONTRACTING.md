# 12 — AGENCY / SUBCONTRACT ANGLE (PHASE 11)

## WHY THIS DESERVES SEPARATE ATTENTION

Steven's constraint is not skill. It is not tools. It is not effort.
**It is customer acquisition**, and every piece of evidence in this mission
points at it:

- 27 Connects ≈ **4 proposals**. His acquisition budget is functionally zero.
- No reviews, no portfolio, no referral network.
- Two proposals out, zero customers.
- Entry-tier marketplace economics (median **$33** fixed-price task) mean
  even a *win* barely pays.

Subcontracting is the only model in this document where **someone else has
already solved the acquisition problem** and is willing to share the revenue
for capacity. That is not a minor advantage. It is the difference between a
plan that can work and a plan that requires Steven to become a salesperson
first.

## THE STRUCTURAL ARGUMENT

Small agencies have a permanent, predictable shape:

1. Revenue depends on billable build hours.
2. Every project generates **non-billable delivery tail** — documentation,
   QA, handover, research, cleanup.
3. The tail is done badly, late, or not at all, by the most expensive person
   in the company, at the worst time of the week.
4. Bad handover docs → more support tickets → worse retention. It costs them
   real money, and they know it.
5. They will not hire a full-time person for it. They *will* pay a
   contractor per unit.

**That gap is exactly Steven's A-grade cluster.** Documentation, QA,
research, explanation. Not his grade-B technical cluster — which is
fortunate, because the grade-B cluster is what he'd otherwise be tempted to
sell.

## WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS (and what it doesn't)

**Confirmed live (Sept 2026):** active hiring threads for freelance/agency
automation help (n8n / Make / APIs, remote) in the n8n community; active
"for hire" posts openly advertising **white-label support to agencies**;
2026 operator content describing automation-agency cost structure as "your
time and any subcontractors." White-label matching demonstrably happens in
the open, in venues Steven can access for free.

**NOT confirmed:** rates for back-end research/QA/documentation subcontract
work. These are privately negotiated and no reliable public table exists.
**$20-40/hr is a working assumption, not evidence.**
→ Antigravity Mission 2 exists to close this. Do not quote it as fact.

## TARGET AGENCY TYPES, RANKED FOR STEVEN

| Rank | Type | Why | Steven's wedge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Automation agencies** (Make/Zapier/n8n, 2-15 people) | Ship fast, document never; Steven speaks enough of the language to be useful | Handover docs, QA passes |
| 2 | **Small consultancies / ops consultants** | Sell process improvement, hate writing it up | SOPs, process maps, research |
| 3 | **Marketing agencies** | Constant research need, thin margins on non-billable work | Competitor/market research packs |
| 4 | **VA agencies** | Capacity spikes, low barrier to entry | Overflow production *(low rate — backup only)* |
| 5 | **Research firms / small software shops** | Higher bar, better rates | QA, documentation |

Start with 1 and 2. They have the sharpest pain and the shortest decision
chain (the owner decides, alone, in one conversation).

## THE OFFER LADDER — ordered by ease of saying yes

1. **QA pass on a build before delivery** — $150-300 or $20-35/hr.
   *Lowest liability in the entire document:* Steven finds problems, he
   doesn't create them. Nothing he touches can break. Easiest first yes.
2. **Client handover documentation / SOP set** — $200-400 or $25-40/hr.
   Highest felt pain. **Lead with this in outreach**, even though QA is the
   easier yes — the doc pain is what makes them reply.
3. **Research pack, white-labeled** — $150-400 or $20-35/hr.
4. **Build-assist under review** (Make.com modules, Sheets) — $25-40/hr,
   **hourly only**, only after trust exists.

Rungs 1-3 are all A-grade work. Rung 4 is grade B and is why it's last.

## HOW TO FIND THEM (all free)
- **n8n community jobs board** — confirmed active, both directions.
- **Make.com partner directory** — a public list of exactly the right companies.
- **LinkedIn** — filter for "automation agency", "ops consultant", 2-20 employees.
- **Agency Discord/Slack communities.**
- **Reddit** — r/agency, r/automation, r/nocode, r/msp.
- **Upwork, inverted** — find agencies *hiring* on Upwork and approach them
  directly. Costs no Connects.

## RULES OF ENGAGEMENT
1. **Never compete with them.** Never approach their clients. Ever. One
   violation ends the channel permanently and the agency world talks.
2. **Never single-agency dependent.** Three live relationships minimum, or
   the rate gets compressed and Steven has no counter.
3. **Paid trial, not free work.** $150-300. Free work signals no price.
4. **Bounded scope, in writing, always.**
5. **Turnaround is the differentiator, not price.** Being the person who
   delivers in 48 hours when they expected a week is worth more than being
   $5/hr cheaper — and it is the one thing Steven can control on day one.
6. **Deliver early. Every time.** This is the entire retention strategy.

## HONEST RISKS
- **Rate compression.** They know Steven has no other channel. Mitigate:
  multiple relationships, priced per deliverable rather than per hour where
  possible.
- **Feast/famine.** Agency work arrives in bursts.
- **Invisible labor.** Steven builds no public portfolio — his best work is
  under someone else's name. Mitigate: keep an internal, redacted record.
- **Slow, silent rejection.** Most outreach is ignored. Ten researched
  messages producing zero replies means fix the message; forty producing
  zero means the offer is wrong for the market.
- **The relationship is the asset and it is fragile.** One bad delivery can
  end it. This cuts both ways — it also means one *great* delivery
  compounds faster than anything on a marketplace.

## VERDICT
**Evaluate seriously → verdict: this is Steven's #2 play and his single
highest-ceiling path.**

It is slower to first dollar than Play 1 (2-6 weeks vs 1-4) and it demands
the one thing he hasn't yet done — get a stranger to say yes. But it is the
**only** play among the three that becomes a business rather than a job, and
it is the only one where the acquisition cost is paid once per relationship
instead of once per job.

Run it in parallel with Play 1 from day one. Play 1 buys the calm needed to
do Play 2 properly, without panic-pricing.
