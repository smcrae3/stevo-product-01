## DEFINITIONS — no wiggle room

| Term | Counts only when |
|---|---|
| **Proposal sent** | Submitted, Connects actually spent |
| **Buyer reply** | A human responded with something other than a rejection |
| **Interview** | A live conversation with a decision-maker occurred |
| **Offer** | Specific work at a specific price was formally offered |
| **Customer** | **Money has cleared into Steven's account** |
| **Revenue** | Cleared, after platform fees. Not invoiced. Not promised. Not in escrow. |
| **Hours acquiring** | Searching, writing, applying, calling, following up, researching targets |
| **Hours delivering** | Only work a client is actually paying for |

**Explicitly NOT metrics** — these are the fake-progress traps:
proposals sent (an input, not an output), files created, videos watched,
tools learned, courses completed, documents written, hours "working," ideas
generated, this report.

> Writing this report is not progress. Getting paid is progress.

## WEEKLY REVIEW — five questions, 15 minutes, Sunday

1. **What actually produced money this week?** (Not what felt productive.)
2. **Which channel had the best reply rate?** Move hours toward it.
3. **What did I do that produced nothing?** Stop doing it.
4. **Did I spend any Connects? Was it justified by the 6/6 rule in `14`?**
5. **Is my True $/hr above $12?** If not, what specifically is dragging it —
   acquisition time, delivery time, or price?

---

## THE THREE HONESTY RULES

1. **A proposal is not a customer.** Steven has sent two proposals and has
   zero customers. Both facts stay on the board until one changes.
2. **Pending is not paid.** Escrow is not cleared. A verbal yes is not cash.
3. **Count the losses.** Every unsuccessful proposal, unanswered message,
   and silent application goes in the acquisition-hours column. That is what
   makes True $/hr true.
