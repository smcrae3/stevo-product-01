# Phase 4 — Offer Pressure Test

Evaluated against Phase 2 (bottleneck ranking) and Phase 3 (verified buyer
behavior on Upwork). No offer is assumed correct by default, including the
ones already "live" or "established."

## A. $99 Automation Audit
**Best for:** a buyer who suspects something is inefficient but can't name
exactly what, or hasn't decided automation is worth paying for yet. Low
commitment, low risk — fits the "small fixed-price, low-risk" pattern that
converts unreviewed freelancers (Phase 3). Good as a genuine first touch
with a cold Upwork buyer who has zero reason to trust Steven yet.
**Bad for:** a buyer whose job post already names a specific broken
workflow and clearly wants it *fixed*, not documented. Pitching a diagnostic
report to someone asking for a fix reads as a delay tactic and a missed
opportunity to quote the actual work.
**Verdict:** keep, and narrow its use — it's a trust-builder for vague or
skeptical buyers, not the default response to every posting.

## B. $199 Trades Lead Response System
**Best for:** a buyer already identified (through warm introduction or a
CSRA relationship) as having the specific, well-documented pain quantified
in Phase 3 — missed calls costing real money, no follow-up system. Works
best where some trust already exists before the price is mentioned, because
it's a bigger ask than $99 and the buyer needs enough context to see why
$199 solves a $45K–$120K/year problem.
**Bad for:** a cold, first-contact Upwork proposal to a stranger with zero
reviews on the seller's side. It's too large a leap of faith for a first
touch, and Phase 3's research suggests actual trades buyers are a small
slice of who's even posting Upwork automation jobs. Using it as the lead
offer on Upwork specifically is likely mismatched to that channel's buyer
population.
**Verdict:** keep the price and packaging exactly as established — do not
touch it — but treat Upwork as a secondary channel for this specific offer.
It's the right offer for the warm-intro/CSRA channel the baseline already
treats as valid, not for cold Upwork outreach.

## C. $299 "One Workflow Fix" (experimental)
**Best for:** exactly the kind of Upwork job post that names one concrete
broken thing ("my form doesn't email me," "leads sit in a spreadsheet
nobody checks"). Phase 3's research shows Upwork buyers frequently phrase
requests this way, and a bounded, one-workflow scope is easy for a buyer to
understand and approve without a call — which also serves the proposal
engine's "minimize unnecessary meetings" goal (Phase 6).
**Bad for:** a buyer who doesn't yet know what's broken (send them to the
$99 Audit first) or one who needs multiple connected systems (that's
Custom, D).
**Verdict — explicitly not auto-approved, per the mission's own
instruction:** the fit logic above is sound on paper, but there is **no
market evidence yet** that a real buyer has said yes to this price and
framing. It should stay exactly what the baseline already calls it —
experimental, quoted case-by-case, not published as a fixed listing —
until it has been offered live on 2–3 real, well-matched jobs and gotten a
result. This is a testable hypothesis, not a confirmed offer. Recommend
using it as a **quoted price in a proposal**, not (yet) a public
Project Catalog listing.

## D. Custom workflow automation
**Best for:** anything genuinely bigger than one workflow — multiple
connected systems, ongoing maintenance, or scope that doesn't fit A/B/C.
Necessary as a catch-all so a real opportunity isn't turned away just
because it doesn't fit a fixed price.
**Bad for:** using it as the default pitch. It requires a scoping
conversation, which adds friction and delay exactly where the goal is
fewer meetings and faster buyer decisions. Should be the fallback, offered
only when the job clearly doesn't fit a bounded product.

## Which positioning is strongest overall

**Evidence-based conclusion: a combination, matched to channel, not a single
winner.**

- **On Upwork (cold, zero-review context):** lead with the **bounded
  workflow repair framing** — either the $99 Audit (buyer doesn't know
  what's broken) or the $299 experimental Fix quoted directly in the
  proposal (buyer already named something specific) — because both match
  the small-fixed-price, low-buyer-risk pattern that actually converts new
  accounts, and both match how Upwork buyers in this category tend to
  phrase their need.
- **On warm intro / CSRA follow-up (some trust already present):** the
  $199 Trades Lead Response System is the strongest fit — it's a bigger,
  more specific ask that needs the context a cold Upwork proposal can't
  carry in a few hundred words.
- **Custom** stays the fallback for scope that outgrows the above, never
  the opener.

This is a **RECOMMENDATION**, not a confirmed result — it hasn't been
tested against a real buyer yet. Treat it as the default strategy for the
next batch of touches, and revisit after the first 10–15 new touches show
whether it moves the reply rate.
