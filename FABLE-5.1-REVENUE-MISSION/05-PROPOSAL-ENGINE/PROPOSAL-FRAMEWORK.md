# Phase 6 — Proposal Engine

## Honesty note on scope
The mission asked for "individualized proposal drafts for the strongest
verified opportunities found." Phase 3 established that no live Upwork job
posting could be pulled into this session (network access to upwork.com is
blocked). There is nothing to individualize against yet. What's built
instead is the reusable engine itself — a structure plus four ready-to-fill
templates — so that when Steven has a real job post open, turning it into a
tailored proposal is a 5-minute fill-in-the-blanks exercise, not a
from-scratch write.

## The structure (every proposal follows this shape)

1. **Open with their problem, in their words.** First line quotes or
   closely paraphrases the specific thing the buyer said is broken. Never
   open with "I am an experienced automation specialist" — that's the
   generic pattern Phase 2 identified as the most likely cause of zero
   replies.
2. **One sentence naming the fix, in plain language.** No jargon, no
   "leverage synergies." Say what would change.
3. **One credibility line, truthful only.** Reference the actual blueprint
   or sample report (Phase 5 artifacts) as "here's the exact architecture
   I'd use" — never a claim of prior client results, since none exist yet.
   If asked for portfolio/reviews, say plainly this is a newer profile and
   point to the attached sample work instead of dodging the question.
4. **Bounded scope + price, stated plainly.** Match to the Phase 4
   decision: $99 Audit if they haven't diagnosed the problem yet, a quoted
   bounded price (referencing the $299 experimental framing internally,
   without necessarily naming "$299" as a fixed rate) if they've named one
   broken workflow, custom-scoped only if it's genuinely bigger than one
   workflow.
5. **One question, not a meeting request.** Ask something specific enough
   to require them to engage with the actual problem (e.g., "does the form
   already notify anyone when it's submitted, or does someone have to check
   the sheet manually?") — this both shows genuine attention and avoids
   asking for a call before there's any trust to justify one.
6. **No sign-off fluff.** End on the question, not "Looking forward to
   hearing from you!" or a summary restating the whole proposal.

## Rules (apply to every draft, no exceptions)
- Never claim a result, client, or deployment that hasn't happened.
- Never use "I leverage cutting-edge AI to revolutionize your workflows" or
  any variant — this is exactly the generic-AI-consultant language buyers
  filter out.
- Keep it under ~150 words. Long proposals get skimmed or skipped.
- Match urgency to the job post — if it's a fixed small scope, don't invite
  scope creep; if it's genuinely open-ended, say so and propose the Audit
  as the first paid step rather than guessing at a price blind.
- Do not submit. These are drafts for Steven's review and personalization,
  per the mission's explicit instruction not to submit proposals.

## How to use the four templates in this folder
1. Open the real job post.
2. Pick the template matching Phase 4's fit logic (vague pain → Audit;
   one named broken workflow → Workflow Fix; trades buyer via warm
   channel → Lead Response; bigger/multi-system → Custom).
3. Fill every `[BRACKETED]` slot with something specific from the actual
   post — if a slot can't be filled with something true and specific, that
   is itself a signal the job may be a SKIP per the Phase 3 rubric.
4. Re-read once for generic language before sending.
