# 19 — THE 90-MINUTE MONEY BLOCK

One repeatable daily unit. Same shape every weekday, so Steven never has to
decide what to do — he just starts.

---

## THE BLOCK

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **0-15** | **SCAN** | Find and qualify new opportunities. Nothing else. |
| **15-40** | **APPLY** | One high-quality application or proposal. **One.** |
| **40-65** | **OUTREACH** | 3-4 agency or warm messages, personalized |
| **65-80** | **FOLLOW UP** | Reply to everything inbound; chase what's due |
| **80-90** | **LOG** | Update the scoreboard. Write tomorrow's first action. |

**Total: 90 minutes. Timer runs. When a segment ends, it ends.**

---

## SEGMENT RULES

### 0-15 — SCAN (15 min, hard stop)
Run 3-4 saved searches. Capture anything that passes the gates into a list.
**Do not apply to anything during this segment.** Scanning and applying are
different mental modes and mixing them is how 15 minutes becomes 50.

**Saved searches to rotate:**
`SOP writer` · `process documentation specialist` · `operations assistant
remote part time` · `implementation coordinator` · `google sheets` (hourly
only) · `customer experience operations`

**Capture format, one line each:**
`source | title | rate | link | why it fits (5 words)`

**If nothing qualifies, that is a valid outcome.** Move to the next segment
at minute 15 regardless.

### 15-40 — APPLY (25 min, one item)
**One** application or proposal, done properly. Pull the matching résumé
variant from `11`, let AI tailor it, **Steven reads every word**, attach the
proof folder link, send.

**THE 12-MINUTE RULE — the most important rule in this file:**
> **If a single application exceeds 12 minutes of writing, stop and send it.**

An application at 90% quality sent today beats one at 100% sent Thursday.
Perfectionism at this stage is not diligence — it is avoidance wearing a
respectable coat, and it is the most likely way this plan fails.

**The 25 minutes covers:** re-read the posting (3) · tailor (10) · check the
link and attachment (2) · send (1) · log (2) · **and 7 minutes of buffer that
belongs to the next application, not to polishing this one.**

**If time remains → do a second application. Never re-edit the first.**

### 40-65 — OUTREACH (25 min, 3-4 messages)
Agency messages from `06`, or warm-network messages from `24`.
**Each message needs one specific, real detail** about that target. Research
is included in the 25 minutes — roughly 4 minutes research + 2 minutes
writing per message.

**No personalization angle findable in 4 minutes → skip that target and move
to the next.** A generic message is worse than no message: it burns the
target permanently and teaches Steven nothing about whether his offer works.

### 65-80 — FOLLOW UP (15 min)
In priority order:
1. **Anyone who replied** — respond same day, always. This is the highest-
   value 15 minutes in the entire block.
2. Follow-up #1 due (5-7 days out)
3. Follow-up #2 due (10-14 days out, then stop forever)
4. Anything owed to a live client

**Replies always outrank new outreach.** If a reply needs the whole block,
give it the whole block — a live conversation is worth more than twenty
cold messages.

### 80-90 — LOG (10 min)
Update the scoreboard in `17`. Then write **one sentence**: tomorrow's first
action.

That sentence removes the hardest part of tomorrow — starting. Steven should
open the block already knowing what minute one is.

---

## ANTI-PERFECTIONISM RULES

**1. THE 12-MINUTE RULE.** One application, 12 minutes of writing, send.

**2. THE TWO-DRAFT RULE.** Draft it, revise it once, send it. **There is no
third draft.** Not for applications, not for outreach, not for proposals.

**3. THE TIMER IS THE BOSS.** Set it per segment. When it rings, move — even
mid-sentence. The block completing matters more than any one item in it.

**4. NO RESEARCH RABBIT HOLES.** Can't find what's needed in 4 minutes? Move
on. There are always more targets.

**5. GOOD ENOUGH IS THE STANDARD.** The failure mode here is not sending bad
work — it is sending *nothing* while polishing. Steven's natural quality
floor is high; the risk runs entirely in the other direction.

**6. NEVER RE-OPEN A SENT ITEM.** It's gone. Learn from the reply, not from
re-reading it.

## VARIANTS FOR REAL LIFE

### THE 45-MINUTE BLOCK (hard day, exhausted, late)
| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0-5 | Scan |
| 5-20 | One application |
| 20-35 | 2 outreach messages |
| 35-42 | Follow up |
| 42-45 | Log |

**Use this freely.** A 45-minute block done is worth infinitely more than a
90-minute block skipped. The habit is the asset.

### THE 15-MINUTE MINIMUM (genuinely no time)
Reply to anything inbound. Send one message. Log it. Done.
**Never zero.** A 15-minute day keeps the chain alive; a zero day makes the
next zero day easier.

### THE DELIVERY BLOCK (paid work exists)
**Paid work outranks the standard block, always.** Client work first, then:
5 min follow-up, 5 min log. Acquisition resumes when delivery is done.

### THE WEEKEND DEEP BLOCK (2-3 hrs)
| Time | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0-90 | **Deep work**: proof piece, AI eval batch, or agency research |
| 90-120 | 3-4 applications |
| 120-150 | 5-6 outreach messages |
| 150-165 | Follow-ups + log |

Only the weekend blocks are long enough for proof pieces and AI eval batches.
**Protect them** — they are where the compounding work happens.

## WEEKLY RHYTHM

| Day | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Mon | **Outreach-heavy** — messages land best early in the week |
| Tue | Applications |
| Wed | AI eval work |
| Thu | Applications + follow-ups |
| Fri | AI eval + close open loops before the weekend |
| Sat | **Deep block** |
| Sun | Deep block + **20-min weekly review** |

---

## THE SUNDAY REVIEW (20 min — the only meeting Steven has)

Five questions:
1. **What produced money this week?** *(Not what felt productive.)*
2. **Which channel had the best reply rate?** → move hours there
3. **What produced nothing?** → stop doing it
4. **Did I spend any Connects? Was it justified?**
5. **Is true $/hr above $15?** If not, what's dragging — acquisition time,
   delivery time, or price?

Then update the one-line status:
> **As of [date]: [N] customers. $[X] cleared. True $/hr: $[Y]. Best channel:
> [name]. Next action: [one thing].**

---

## THE ONE THING THAT MAKES THIS WORK

> **Same time. Same place. Every weekday.**

The block's power isn't the 90 minutes — it's the removal of daily
negotiation. Steven should not be deciding each evening whether he has the
energy. The block starts at [time]; he sits down; the timer runs.

**Miss a day → do not double up tomorrow.** That is how people quit. Just do
the next block.
