# 01 — THE FOUR CASH ENGINES

Four engines, deliberately independent. Each has a different buyer, a
different acquisition channel, and a different failure mode — so one dying
does not touch the others.

**Capacity reality:** Steven works full time. Total available: **8-12 hrs/week.**
Allocation below is not aspirational, it is a budget.

| | E1 AI Eval | E2 Agency Subcontract | E3 Hourly Contract/Freelance | E4 Productized |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hrs/wk allocated | 4-6 | 2-3 | 1-2 (applying) | 1 |
| First dollar | 2-5 wks | 3-8 wks | 4-10 wks | 1-6 wks |
| Rate | $17-30/hr | $20-40/hr | $22-47/hr | $75-300/job |
| Acquisition cost | **$0** | $0 + hours | **$0** | $0 |
| Role | **Cash floor** | **The business** | **The exit** | **Proof + fast cash** |

## ENGINE 1 — AI EVALUATION / TRAINING

**WHO PAYS.** AI data vendors staffing frontier labs: **Mercor, Surge AI,
Outlier (Scale AI), Handshake AI, Alignerr (Labelbox), DataAnnotation,
Invisible Technologies, Micro1, Prolific, TELUS International AI,
Clickworker, OneForma, CrowdGen, Toloka.**

**WHY THEY PAY.** Human judgment is the product. Models are trained and
graded on it, labs are supply-constrained on careful generalists who can
*write* a defensible critique, and no amount of automation removes the need
for a human to say "this answer is subtly wrong, and here is why."

**WHAT STEVEN DOES.** Reads two model outputs. Decides which is better
against *their* rubric, not his taste. Writes 60-150 words a reviewer can
audit. Checks claimed facts. Flags ambiguity instead of guessing. Stays
consistent on item 200 the way he was on item 1.

**WHAT AI DOES.** Almost nothing — and that is the point. AI helps only in
Steven's own prep: understanding a rubric, practicing critique structure.
**Using AI to generate his evaluations would be fraud and is instant
permanent removal across the industry.**

**FINAL OUTPUT.** Ratings, rankings, written critiques, factuality flags,
logged hours.

**RATE.** $17-30/hr generalist; $30-55/hr on RLHF/preference tiers with a
quality record. Industry average ~$31/hr. Specialist tiers ($85-160/hr) are
credential-gated and **out of scope**.

**TIME TO FIRST DOLLAR.** 2-5 weeks. Outlier pays weekly (Tuesdays);
Handshake's first payout is 2-4 weeks. Screening is 50-60 min (Handshake) to
1-5 hrs (Outlier, where **qualification is paid at the work rate**).

**ACQUISITION CHANNEL.** Direct application. No selling, ever. This is the
entire reason it is Engine 1.

**DELIVERY RISK.** Low. Quality scores can drop and end a project.
Mitigate: slow down, re-read the rubric, never rush a batch.

**CREDIBILITY RISK.** Low — nothing to misrepresent. The one real danger is
**overclaiming a specialist tier** Steven isn't qualified for. Getting caught
ends every platform at once, because these vendors share reputation signals.

**PROOF REQUIRED.** **None.** No portfolio, no reviews, no references. This
is the only engine with a zero-proof entry, which is why it goes first.

**THE REAL RISK, NAMED.** **Throughput, not rate.** A $30/hr project offering
4 hrs/week pays less than a $20/hr project offering 12. Rate is contractual;
hours are not. **Mitigation is non-negotiable: onboard to 4+ platforms.**

## ENGINE 2 — AGENCY SUBCONTRACT / WHITE-LABEL BACKEND PRODUCTION

**WHO PAYS.** Owners of 2-15 person automation agencies, no-code agencies,
marketing-ops shops, small RevOps and process consultancies, VA agencies,
content-ops firms.

**WHY THEY PAY.** Revenue depends on billable build hours. Every project
generates a non-billable tail — handover docs, QA, research, cleanup — done
badly, late, by the most expensive person in the company, on a Friday night.
Bad handover docs generate support tickets and cost retention. They will not
hire full-time for it. They **will** pay a contractor per unit.

**WHAT STEVEN DOES.** Outreach. Scoping calls (his strongest single skill).
Interviews their builder. Verifies every step against the real system.
Writes to their template and brand. Delivers early. Never ships AI output
he hasn't personally checked.

**WHAT AI DOES.** Heavy lifting on first drafts, research synthesis, and
structure. **The draft is raw material, never the deliverable.** Steven's
verification pass is what he is actually paid for.

**FINAL OUTPUT.** White-labeled SOPs, client handover packets, QA bug
sheets, test-case sets, research packs, cleaned datasets — on the agency's
template, under the agency's brand.

**RATE.** $25-40/hr documentation; $20-35/hr QA and research; or $150-400
per bounded deliverable. *(Estimate — no public rate table exists for this
work. AG-3 validates it.)*

**TIME TO FIRST DOLLAR.** 3-8 weeks at 2-3 hrs/week of outreach.

**ACQUISITION CHANNEL.** Direct outreach into partner directories and
communities. **Ten researched messages beat a hundred generic ones.**

**DELIVERY RISK.** Medium. Scope creep, brand-voice mismatch, and
unverified AI content are the three failure modes. All three are handled by
the SOP in `03`.

**CREDIBILITY RISK.** **The highest of the four engines.** Agency owners can
tell in one call whether someone has done this before. The defense is not
bluffing — it is leading with the two proof pieces and being visibly honest
about the boundary: *"I'm not your build engineer. I make what your engineer
built explainable, tested, and documented."*

**PROOF REQUIRED.** Two artifacts, buildable in ~3 hours: **one sample SOP**
and **one sample QA bug sheet**, both labeled DEMONSTRATION. That is the
entire portfolio gate. See `07`.

## ENGINE 3 — HOURLY CONTRACT / FREELANCE OPERATIONS + SPREADSHEET WORK

**WHO PAYS.** SMBs, startups, agencies, healthcare/logistics ops teams
hiring part-time or contract remote staff; plus hourly Upwork clients.

**WHY THEY PAY.** They need a specific gap covered — process undocumented,
ops person overloaded, spreadsheets a mess, a rollout needs coordinating —
and they don't want a full-time hire.

**WHAT STEVEN DOES.** Applies consistently (5/day, timeboxed). Interviews
well — his strongest asset in a hiring context. Once in: process interviews,
SOP writing, ops admin, Sheets work, reporting.

**WHAT AI DOES.** Tailors applications fast (10 quality applications in the
time 3 used to take). Drafts SOPs once employed. Steven reads everything
before it goes out.

**FINAL OUTPUT.** Timesheets and completed work products.

**RATE.** SOP/process writer **$28.85-$47.12/hr** (avg $38.94) · operations
assistant **$15.38-$22.36/hr** (avg $19.82) · implementation support
~$20-30/hr · hourly Sheets freelance ~$25-35/hr *(Steven's own live data
point: he bid $28/hr on the Sheets merge job)*.

**TIME TO FIRST DOLLAR.** 4-10 weeks. Hiring cycles are slow and this cannot
be rushed.

**ACQUISITION CHANNEL.** Direct application — Indeed, ZipRecruiter,
LinkedIn, Wellfound, staffing firms. **Free.**

**DELIVERY RISK.** Low once hired.

**CREDIBILITY RISK.** Medium — résumé has no matching job title. Fixed by
the reframes in `11` and the same two proof pieces.

**PROOF REQUIRED.** The same sample SOP. **One artifact serves Engines 2 and
3.** That is why it is Day 1.

## ENGINE 4 — PRODUCTIZED $75-$300 SERVICES

**WHO PAYS.** Warm network, local CSRA businesses, small agencies, Fiverr
and Upwork buyers.

**WHY THEY PAY.** Something is broken or blocking them *today* and the price
is low enough to decide alone, without a meeting.

**WHAT STEVEN DOES.** Intake, delivery, verification, a clear handover note.

**WHAT AI DOES.** Compresses 5-8 hours of reading/structuring/drafting into
1-3. This is the whole margin.

**FINAL OUTPUT.** A working sheet, a written SOP, a clean dataset, a
research brief, a live intake form.

**PRICE.** $75-$300. **Never below $75** — intake and comms eat the margin.

**TIME TO FIRST DOLLAR.** 1-6 weeks. Via warm network, potentially days.

**ACQUISITION CHANNEL.** Warm network first (highest conversion, zero cost),
then local, then Fiverr (free to list, works in the background forever).

**DELIVERY RISK.** **Scope creep is the killer.** One named problem per
fixed price. "Fix my sheet" is not a scope; "the pivot on tab 3 breaks when
rows are added" is.

**CREDIBILITY RISK.** Low with warm buyers, high with cold ones.

**PROOF REQUIRED.** One before/after artifact per service.

## HOW THEY INTERLOCK

- **Two proof pieces unlock three engines.** Sample SOP → E2 + E3 + E4.
  Sample QA sheet → E2. Three hours of work, the highest leverage in the pack.
- **E1 funds the patience** to run E2 and E3 without panic-pricing.
- **E4 produces the case study** that makes E2's outreach credible.
- **E2 is the only engine that becomes a business.** E1 and E3 are jobs.
  E4 is a business with an acquisition problem.
- **Failure independence:** E1 throughput dries up → E2/E3/E4 untouched.
  Agency outreach flops → E1 still pays. No hiring bites → E1 + E4 continue.

## WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT AN ENGINE

**Building automations for direct clients.** It is where Steven's enthusiasm
points, it is grade-B skill with unbounded debugging, and it carries
liability he cannot price or fix at 2am. It appears in this pack **only** as
supervised hourly work (E3) or as agency build-assist under review (E2) —
never as a fixed-price offer he owns alone.
