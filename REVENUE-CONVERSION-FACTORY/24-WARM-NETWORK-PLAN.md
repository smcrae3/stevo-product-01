# 24 — WARM NETWORK STRATEGY

## WHY THIS IS FIRST, NOT LAST

Warm network is the **only channel with a realistic chance of producing cash
this week**, it converts **5-10x better than any cold channel**, and it costs
**zero dollars and zero Connects**.

It is also the one Steven is most likely to skip — because it feels like
asking for a favor, and because if it goes badly he has to see these people
again.

**So the entire design goal here is: make the ask carry no social weight.**

---

## THE CORE PRINCIPLE

> **Announce what you do. Do not ask for work.**

These are completely different messages:

| Begging | Announcing |
|---|---|
| "Do you know anyone who could hire me?" | "I do X now — if you know anyone with Y, send them my way." |
| Puts the burden on them to solve his problem | Gives them information they can use or ignore |
| Requires a yes/no answer | Requires nothing |
| Awkward if they can't help | Fine if they can't help |
| Can only be sent once | Can be repeated naturally over time |

The second version is how professionals talk about their work. It also
happens to be the version that gets forwarded.

## THE THREE RULES
1. **No hype.** No "excited to announce," no rocket emojis, no "AI-powered."
2. **Be specific about the work.** "Spreadsheet cleanup and writing down how
   things get done" beats "business consulting" — vague offers get zero
   referrals because nobody can pattern-match them to a person they know.
3. **Always give them an easy out.** "No worries if nothing comes to mind" —
   it removes obligation, which is exactly what makes people willing to
   actually think about it.

---

## SEGMENT 1 — FORMER T-MOBILE COLLEAGUES
*(The best segment. They know he's competent and there's no current
workplace awkwardness.)*

> Hey [Name] — hope you're doing well. Random update: I've been doing
> freelance work on the side — spreadsheet cleanup, writing SOPs and process
> docs, and QA testing for small businesses and agencies.
>
> If you ever run into someone whose spreadsheets are a mess, or who's trying
> to document a process nobody's written down, I'd appreciate you thinking of
> me. First small job is $95 so it's easy for someone to try.
>
> No worries if nothing comes to mind — mostly just letting people know.
> How's [specific thing you know about them]?

**Why it works:** short, specific, no ask beyond awareness, ends with genuine
interest in them.

**Bonus ask for colleagues who moved into ops/PM/BA roles:**
> Also — I noticed you moved into [role]. I'm trying to get into operations
> and process work myself. Would you be up for 15 minutes sometime to tell me
> how you made that jump? No agenda, just useful for me.

**People say yes to this almost always.** It costs them nothing, it flatters
them appropriately, and it produces referrals more often than a direct ask
does.

---

## SEGMENT 2 — CURRENT TRUSTED COWORKERS
*(Careful — Steven has a full-time job. Only people he genuinely trusts.)*

> Hey — keeping this between us, but I've been building up some freelance
> work on the side. Spreadsheet cleanup, process documentation, that kind of
> thing. Nothing that conflicts with here.
>
> If you know anyone outside work who needs that sort of help, I'd appreciate
> the pointer. And if you'd rather not be involved, totally understood.

**Rules for this segment:** only people who will not repeat it · never during
work hours or on company systems · nothing that could look like competing
with his employer · **when in doubt, skip the person.** The downside here is
asymmetric — a lost referral costs nothing, a workplace problem costs the
income that funds everything else.

---

## SEGMENT 3 — FRIENDS AND FAMILY
*(Casual, in person or by text.)*

> Hey — side thing I've been doing: I clean up messy spreadsheets and write
> up how businesses do things so they're not relying on one person's memory.
> Sounds boring, but people pay decent money for it because nobody wants to
> do it themselves.
>
> If anyone you know ever complains about that stuff, point them at me.

**In person, even shorter:**
> "I've been doing freelance spreadsheet and documentation work on the side.
> If you ever hear someone complaining about a broken spreadsheet, that's
> literally my thing now."

**Say it once and move on.** Don't over-explain — over-explaining reads as
needing something.

---

## SEGMENT 4 — LOCAL BUSINESS OWNERS HE ALREADY KNOWS
*(Mechanic, barber, dentist, gym, restaurant, church contacts. Highest
conversion of any segment.)*

**In person, conversationally:**
> "Random question — do you track [appointments / jobs / customers] in a
> spreadsheet? I do that kind of work on the side now. Cleaning them up,
> fixing them when they break, setting up forms so stuff doesn't get lost.
> If yours ever gives you trouble, I'm around."

**Then stop talking.** Let them respond. If they show interest, one follow-up
question:
> "What's the most annoying part of it right now?"

That question does the entire sale. They describe the pain; Steven names the
$95 fix. **No pitch required.**

---

## SEGMENT 5 — LINKEDIN CONNECTIONS

**Individual DM to relevant 1st-degree connections:**
> Hi [Name] — hope things are good at [Company]. Quick note: I've started
> taking freelance work in process documentation, QA testing, and spreadsheet
> operations, mostly for small teams and agencies.
>
> If [Company] ever needs handover docs written or a build QA'd before it
> ships, or if you know someone who does, I'd appreciate the thought. Samples
> here if useful: [link].
>
> Either way, hope you're well.

**One public post (do this once, in week 1):**
> I've started taking freelance work alongside my day job.
>
> Three things: process documentation and SOPs, QA testing for workflows and
> automations, and spreadsheet cleanup and data work.
>
> The through-line is my background in customer operations — I spent years
> finding out what written procedures leave out, and what real users do to a
> system that nobody tested for.
>
> I put together some work samples. They're demonstrations rather than client
> work, because I'm being straight about where I am: [link]
>
> If you know a small agency drowning in handover docs, or anyone with a
> spreadsheet nobody understands — I'd appreciate the introduction.

**Why the honesty line lands:** "demonstrations rather than client work,
because I'm being straight about where I am" is disarming, credible, and
memorable. It reads as confidence, not weakness — and it means nothing can
later be caught out.

---

## SEGMENT 6 — THE REFERRAL-ASK FOLLOW-UP
*(For anyone who replied warmly but had nobody in mind.)*

> Thanks [Name], appreciate it. One more thing if it's easy — is there anyone
> specific you'd think of who runs a small business or manages an office? Even
> just a name is helpful; I'm not asking you to make an introduction.

**"Even just a name" is the whole trick.** People freeze at "do you know
anyone?" because it's an open search across their entire memory. A named
category is answerable in three seconds.

---

## THE CAMPAIGN — 25 asks over 2 weeks, ~2 hours total

| Week | Segment | Count | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, Day 1 | Former T-Mobile colleagues | 10 | 20 min |
| 1, Day 2 | Friends & family | 10 | 15 min |
| 1, Day 3 | LinkedIn post + 5 DMs | 5 | 25 min |
| 2 | Local business owners he knows, in person | 5 | ongoing |
| 2 | Follow up on every warm reply | — | 20 min |

**Target: 25 asks. Expect 3-5 real conversations and 1-2 leads.**

**Realistic outcome: 1 paying customer within 2-3 weeks.** That single
customer produces the testimonial and case study that make every other
channel in this pack more credible.

---

## THE FOLLOW-THROUGH RULES

1. **Reply to every response the same day.** Someone who took the time to
   answer is the warmest lead Steven has.
2. **Never chase twice.** One message, one follow-up if they engaged, then
   stop. These are relationships that outlast this campaign.
3. **Thank every referrer, whether or not it converts.** Then tell them what
   happened. People refer again when they learn the outcome; they go quiet
   when their referral disappears into silence.
4. **Deliver spectacularly to the first warm customer.** Under-promise,
   over-deliver, deliver early. **A warm customer's bad experience travels
   through the network that produced them** — this is the one place where a
   mediocre delivery has compounding downside.
5. **Re-announce naturally every few months.** Not a campaign — just
   mentioning a project he's working on when it comes up. Most referrals
   arrive on the second or third exposure, not the first.

---

## THE THING TO GET OVER

Steven will feel that this is embarrassing — that telling people he does
freelance spreadsheet work is admitting something.

It isn't, and the framing is wrong. **He is not asking for help. He is
telling people what he does now.** That is what every self-employed person
does, permanently, forever. The alternative — hoping strangers on a
marketplace discover him while he has 14 Connects — is objectively the harder
path, and he has already tested it four times.

> **20 messages. One hour. Zero dollars. The highest expected-value hour in
> the entire 30-day plan.**
