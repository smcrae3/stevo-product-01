# 15 — CLIENT ACCEPTANCE GATES

Six reusable gates. Each is a checklist Steven runs **before** signing, in
addition to the profitability calculator in `14`. The rulebook answers *"is
this worth doing?"*; these gates answer *"is this safe to do?"*

**Universal walk-away flags — apply to all six:**
- Asks Steven to pay anything to start work
- Wants to move off-platform to avoid payment protection *(before any
  relationship exists)*
- Unpaid "test" longer than 30 minutes
- Pressure to start before terms are agreed
- Refuses to define what "done" means
- Hostile, dismissive, or contemptuous in the first conversation *(this
  predicts the whole engagement — believe it)*
- Steven cannot explain the delivery plan out loud in three sentences

---

## GATE 1 — RESEARCH

**MUST KNOW BEFORE ACCEPTING**
- The **exact question** being answered — one sentence, in writing
- How the output will be **used** (decision input? published? client-facing?)
- **Source constraints** — public only? paywalled DBs? Are those provided?
- What "**enough**" looks like: how many sources, how many rows, how deep
- Format and template
- Who reviews it and what they'll challenge

**MUST SEE**
- An example of a past deliverable they liked *(the single best scope-setter
  available — ask for it every time)*
- Their template, if one exists
- A sample of any data being provided

**MUST TEST**
- Do a **15-minute sample search** before quoting. If the sources are thin,
  paywalled, or don't exist, the job is 3x harder than it looks and this is
  where Steven finds out — for free.

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- **Hourly, whenever the source list is open.** Non-negotiable.
- Fixed only when the deliverable is countable *(e.g. "8 competitors, this
  template, these 5 fields")* and ≤4 hrs
- Milestones ≤$300
- 50% upfront on any fixed research over $200

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- "Just find everything you can about X" — infinite scope
- Regulated domain (medical, legal, financial advice)
- They want a specific *conclusion* rather than an answer
- Requires paywalled access they won't provide
- Fixed price + open sources → **this is the exact $9.60/hr shape. Decline.**

## GATE 2 — SPREADSHEET

**MUST KNOW**
- The **one named problem**, or the exact definition of "clean"
- Row and column count, number of tabs
- Who else edits this file and whether it's live right now
- Is it connected to anything downstream (scripts, imports, dashboards)?
- Are there formulas Steven must not break?

**MUST SEE**
- **The actual file, or a representative sample, before quoting.** Never
  quote on a description. A "small cleanup" of 400 rows and a "small
  cleanup" of 40,000 are different jobs.
- The messiest section, specifically

**MUST TEST**
- Open it. Check for merged cells, hidden tabs, array formulas, external
  links, and volatile functions — the five things that turn 2 hours into 8.
- **Duplicate the file before touching anything.** Always.

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- Fixed acceptable for a **named single problem** ($95-150)
- Hourly for anything open-ended or over ~2,000 rows
- 100% on delivery for jobs under $150; 50% upfront above that

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- Won't share the file before quoting
- "Just make it better" with no definition
- Live production file with multiple concurrent editors and no backup
- Financial or payroll data where an error has monetary consequence
- Wants Steven to guarantee a specific outcome from bad source data

---

## GATE 3 — AUTOMATION

**This gate is deliberately the strictest. Automation is where Steven's
skill is grade B and the liability is grade A.**

**MUST KNOW**
- Exactly what triggers it, what it does, what it outputs
- What happens **when it fails** — and who notices
- Whose accounts and credentials are involved
- Is this touching a live business process people depend on today?
- Who maintains it after handover?
- What is the cost of it silently doing the wrong thing for a week?

**MUST SEE**
- The existing system and any current automation
- Real sample data
- The accounts/permissions involved

**MUST TEST**
- **Build the riskiest step first, in a sandbox, before committing to a
  price.** If Steven can't get that step working with AI assistance in an
  hour, he cannot do the job — and finding that out in hour one costs
  nothing.

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- **HOURLY ONLY. Always. No exceptions.** Debugging is unbounded and Steven
  cannot cap his own downside on grade-B skill.
- Weekly invoicing on anything over 10 hours
- If a client insists on fixed price → decline or restructure to a paid
  discovery phase first

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- Fixed price of any size
- Production system, no supervision, no rollback
- Requires n8n, CRM implementation, or custom API development
- "It should be simple" *(from someone who hasn't built it)*
- Money movement, payroll, medical, or legal data in the pipeline
- They want Steven **on call** for failures
- No test environment and no willingness to create one

**THE HONEST SENTENCE — say it early, it wins more work than it loses**
> "I build simple Sheets and Make.com automations with AI assistance and I
> test them thoroughly. I'm not an engineer, so I work hourly on this and
> I'll tell you within the first hour if it's beyond me — before you've spent
> real money."

## GATE 4 — SOP / DOCUMENTATION

**MUST KNOW**
- **Who reads this** — new hire, VA, client, auditor? *(This changes everything.)*
- How many processes *(each additional is a separate job)*
- Is the process stable, or changing while Steven documents it?
- Does an approver exist, and are they available?
- Screenshots required? Any that can't be captured for privacy reasons?
- Is this documenting what they **do**, or what they **should** do?
  *(Two different jobs — the second is consulting.)*

**MUST SEE**
- A recorded walkthrough, **or** access to the person who does the work
- Any existing partial documentation
- A past doc they liked, if one exists

**MUST TEST**
- Can Steven actually get access to perform the steps himself? **If he can't
  verify by doing, he can only transcribe** — and that's a cheaper, lower-
  value job that should be priced and named as such.

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- Fixed fine for **one process** at $200-350 — this is the one category where
  fixed price genuinely works, because the deliverable is naturally bounded
- Hourly for multi-process or discovery-heavy work
- One revision included, stated in writing; further rounds $75/hr

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- Regulated compliance documentation (FDA, HIPAA, OSHA, financial audit) —
  liability far beyond the fee
- The person who knows the process is unavailable or uncooperative
- They want the process *improved* but are paying to have it documented
- Unlimited revisions
- "Document all our processes" for one flat fee

---

## GATE 5 — AI EVALUATION

**MUST KNOW**
- Rate, and whether onboarding/qualification is paid
- **Expected weekly volume, and how stable it has been** *(the single most
  important question — rate without hours is meaningless)*
- How quality is scored and whether Steven can see his score
- Payment schedule and first-payout timing
- Whether any fee is charged to him *(any fee = disqualified, immediately)*

**MUST SEE**
- The rubric, before starting
- Published payment terms
- The task interface

**MUST TEST**
- Complete 3 calibration items slowly, re-read the rubric, compare his
  judgments. **Never batch-work before calibrating.**

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- Platform terms, non-negotiable at entry. **Don't negotiate on contract #1** —
  leverage here comes from a quality record.

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- Any fee charged to the worker
- Rate undisclosed until after unpaid work
- Vague or unpublished payment terms
- Requires credentials Steven doesn't have → **never misrepresent to reach a
  specialist tier.** These vendors share reputation signals; getting caught
  ends all of them at once.
- Credible non-payment or mass-deactivation reports *(AG-4 checks this)*

---

## GATE 6 — AGENCY SUBCONTRACTING

**MUST KNOW**
- Exactly what the deliverable is, and **who reads it**
- Whose brand and template
- What access is needed — and explicitly, **what must Steven not touch**
- Who reviews his work before it reaches their client
- The real deadline behind the stated one
- Payment terms and schedule
- **Is Steven ever client-facing?** If yes, under what terms?

**MUST SEE**
- A past deliverable of the type requested that they were happy with
- Their template and brand guidelines
- The build or material he'll be working from

**MUST TEST**
- **One small paid pilot ($150-300) before any larger commitment.** This
  tests them as much as it tests Steven — how they scope, how they
  communicate, whether they pay on time.

**PAYMENT CONDITIONS**
- Net-15 or net-30 maximum on a first engagement
- Per-deliverable or hourly; **hourly whenever scope isn't fully bounded**
- Paid pilot first, always. **Never free work** — free signals no price and
  attracts agencies that never convert.
- Invoice immediately on delivery, not monthly, until they've paid twice

**WALK-AWAY FLAGS**
- Wants free "sample" work beyond ~30 minutes
- Payment terms beyond net-30, or "we pay when the client pays"
  *(their cash-flow risk is not Steven's)*
- Wants him client-facing under their brand without agreed terms
- Production access with no supervision
- Pushes fixed price on open-ended scope
- Asks him to misrepresent his experience to their client
- **Any suggestion he approach their clients directly** — walk away
  immediately and permanently

**THE RELATIONSHIP RULES**
1. Never contact their clients. Ever.
2. Three live relationships minimum — single-agency dependence compresses the
   rate with no counter.
3. Turnaround is the differentiator, not price.
4. Deliver early, every time.

---

## THE ONE-MINUTE VERSION

Before saying yes to anything:

1. **Can I name exactly what "done" means?** No → don't accept yet.
2. **Have I seen the actual thing?** No → don't quote yet.
3. **Is it hourly, or countable and ≤4 hrs?** No → restructure.
4. **What's the worst realistic outcome?** Catastrophic → decline.
5. **True rate ≥ $15/hr?** No → decline.
6. **Can I explain the plan in three sentences?** No → decline.

Six questions, sixty seconds. They would have caught the $225 research job
and the $150 forms job at questions 1, 3, and 5.
