# 23 — CSRA LOCAL MONEY PLAY

**Coverage:** Augusta · Evans · Martinez · Grovetown · North Augusta ·
greater CSRA.

## WHAT LOCAL IS ACTUALLY FOR

Local will not be Steven's biggest revenue line. The realistic ceiling is a
few hundred dollars a month at 2-3 hrs/week.

**Its real value is the case study.** One local client produces:
- A **named, real, referenceable customer** — Steven currently has zero
- A **testimonial he can quote** without inventing anything
- A **before/after artifact** with real business context
- **Referrals**, which is how local actually compounds
- Proof to himself that a stranger will pay him money

Every one of those unlocks Engines 2, 3, and 4. **Treat local as
proof-manufacturing that happens to pay**, and it becomes worth the hours.

**Budget: 2-3 hrs/week, weekends. One bounded experiment, not a campaign.**

## THE BEST 3 OFFERS FOR LOCAL

### OFFER 1 — SHEET RESCUE · **$95** *(founding: $75)*
**Pitch:** "Tell me the one thing your spreadsheet won't do. I'll fix it in
24 hours and explain in plain English what went wrong."
**Why it works locally:** the buyer is blocked *today*, $95 is a decide-alone
amount, and it produces a paying customer in a single afternoon.
**Delivery:** 1-2 hours. **This is the door-opener.**

### OFFER 2 — INTAKE FORM SETUP · **$225** *(founding: $175)*
**Pitch:** "Stop taking job requests by text and sticky note. I'll set up a
form that captures everything you need, drops it into an organized sheet, and
automatically confirms with the customer."
**Why it works locally:** **highly visible and immediately understandable.**
The owner can see it working on their own phone in five minutes. That
tangibility is worth more than any explanation, and it is the offer most
likely to generate a referral.
**Delivery:** 3-5 hours. Includes a 1-page maintenance SOP.

### OFFER 3 — SOP IN 48 HOURS · **$250** *(founding: $150)*
**Pitch:** "Record yourself doing the thing once — 20 minutes, just talk
while you click — and I'll turn it into a written procedure your next hire
can follow on day one."
**Why it works locally:** every small business owner has the "it's all in my
head" problem and most have felt it during a vacation or a resignation.
**Delivery:** 2-4 hours. **Highest margin of the three.**

**The sequence:** $95 Sheet Rescue → free "here's what else looked fragile"
note → $225 form setup or $250 SOP → referral.

## HOW TO APPROACH — NO SPAM, EVER

**Never:** mass email, cold-call lists, LinkedIn blasting, or dropping
flyers. It doesn't work in a small market and it damages a reputation Steven
will need for years. **The CSRA is small enough that being remembered as "the
guy who spammed us" is a real, lasting cost.**

### Route 1 — Warm network first *(highest conversion, start here)*
Before approaching a single stranger, work `24`. Steven almost certainly
already knows someone who owns or manages a small business. **One warm
introduction is worth thirty cold approaches.**

### Route 2 — Existing customer relationships
Businesses Steven already patronizes — his mechanic, his barber, his dentist,
the gym, the local restaurant. He is a known face, not a salesperson. The
conversation is natural:
> "Random question — do you track [appointments/jobs] in a spreadsheet? I do
> that kind of cleanup work on the side now. If it ever breaks, I'm your guy."

**No pitch. Plant it and leave.** Half of these come back weeks later.

### Route 3 — Chamber of Commerce events
Augusta Metro Chamber, Columbia County Chamber (Evans/Martinez), North
Augusta Chamber. **Attend one event before paying for membership.** Go to
listen, not to pitch. Answer the "what do you do?" question well and let
conversations happen.

**The answer to "what do you do?":**
> "I help small businesses with the spreadsheet and paperwork side — cleaning
> up data, setting up intake forms, writing down how things get done so it's
> not all in one person's head."

Concrete, understandable, and it triggers recognition rather than confusion.
**Never say "AI automation."** Nobody at a Chamber breakfast is buying that
from a stranger.

### Route 4 — Local Facebook business groups
Join, then **be useful for three weeks before mentioning services.** Answer
spreadsheet questions for free. Help people. The work sells itself when
someone with a real problem sees a competent, generous answer.

### Route 5 — Nonprofits and churches
Genuinely high-need, referral-dense, and the most forgiving first customers.
Consider **one deliberately discounted or free job** for a nonprofit Steven
already has a connection to — explicitly in exchange for a written
testimonial and permission to use it as a case study. **Once. Named purpose.
Not a pattern.**

## TRUST & PROOF STRATEGY

Steven has no local reputation. Three things build it fast:

**1. The demonstration folder.** Six real artifacts (`07`), all labeled
DEMONSTRATION. He can pull up the SOP or the intake workflow on his phone in
thirty seconds. Showing beats claiming.

**2. Money-back framing on the first job.**
> "If it's not useful, don't pay me."
Costs almost nothing (he'll rarely have to honor it) and eliminates the
buyer's entire risk calculation.

**3. Explain, don't impress.** His actual competitive advantage locally is
that he speaks plain English about technical things. Every competitor either
talks over the owner's head or is a faceless online service. **Being the
person who explains it clearly, in person, in Augusta, is the whole moat.**

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't claim clients he doesn't have. It gets checked in a small market.
- Don't call himself an agency. He's one person and that's fine — for a
  local owner, one accountable person is a *feature*.
- Don't lead with AI. Lead with the outcome.
- Don't price below $75. Cheap reads as unserious.

## THE LOCAL EXPERIMENT — 30 days, ~10 hours total

| Week | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm network only (`24`). No cold approaches. | Included in `24` |
| 2 | Prep: get AG-5's target list; identify 10 businesses Steven already has a real connection to | 1 hr |
| 3 | **Talk to 5 businesses in person.** No pitching — just the "what do you do" answer and a card. | 2-3 hrs |
| 4 | Follow up with the 5. Talk to 5 more. Attend one Chamber event if timing works. | 2-3 hrs |

**Success:** 1 paying local customer at any price, with a testimonial and
permission to use it as a case study.

**Failure signal:** 10 in-person conversations, zero interest → **local is
not Steven's channel.** Stop and reallocate those hours to Engine 2. That is
a legitimate, useful finding, not a defeat — and it costs ten hours to learn
rather than three months.

## WHAT TO SAY WHEN THEY ASK "HOW MUCH?"

> "Depends what it is. If it's one broken thing in a spreadsheet, $95 and
> you'll have it back tomorrow. If it's setting up a proper intake form so
> requests stop getting lost, that's around $225. If you want a process
> written down so you can hand it to someone, $250. I'll tell you which one
> you actually need — and sometimes the answer is that you don't need me."

That last clause closes more work than any amount of enthusiasm, because it
is the one thing a small-business owner never hears from someone selling to
them.
