# 05 — Video Description

AI can find information fast — but it can also sound completely confident
while being wrong. In this video I walk through the actual rule I use
every time I do research with AI help: AI discovers, the source proves
it, and I'm the one who has to check before anything counts as fact.

Using a small, self-directed public-research example (real city water
department pages — nothing private, no client data), I show:

- What AI is actually good for in this process (finding candidate pages
  fast)
- Why I never let AI's summary substitute for opening the real source
  myself
- Why guessing an email address is a bad idea, even when it looks
  plausible
- What I do when the information genuinely isn't public — leave it
  blank, don't invent it
- The simple QA check I run before calling anything "verified"

This isn't a "look what I earned" video — it's a practical look at one
habit that makes AI-assisted research actually trustworthy instead of
just fast.
